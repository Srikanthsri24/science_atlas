## Changes

Optical benches gain mirror-vertex normal/angle guides, metallic rims, carriages and adjustment knobs. Ray markers remain confined to real light paths; virtual extensions carry no light. Plane mirrors gain backing, equal-distance rulers and paired incidence/reflection angles. Refraction gains angular graduations and a total-internal-reflection trial; its readout says “No ray (TIR)” without a degree unit. The reduced eye gains aperture and nerve landmarks while retaining its explicit thin-lens limitations.

Interference and diffraction use aperture widths and centre separations linked to selected inputs. Segmented barriers leave actual openings, incoming markers meet those openings, and outgoing wavefronts start at the same centres. Aperture geometry is enlarged independently of the stated detector scale. A scanning probe reads the calculated intensity profile. Prism mounts, edges and the first-face normal clarify the existing three-wavelength Snell construction and its chosen dispersion law.

Echo and SONAR lessons follow ordered outward/return journeys with range guides. Reverberation adds treatment landmarks and uses a shared physical clock referenced to 20 m² absorption area, so stronger absorption visibly accelerates decay. Paths remain illustrative rather than a calibrated room-ray solver. Doppler framing fits the representative wavefront rings; explicit wavelength guides distinguish compression ahead from spreading behind.

Pendulums gain angle graduations and support clearance at the 4 m limit. Seconds-pendulum trials choose the length appropriate to gravity. Period comparisons isolate length, mass and gravity. The nine-pendulum array gains a base, feet, identifiers and endpoint clock; all pendulums reunite at its 100-second concept endpoint. SHM progresses through equilibrium toward the opposite extreme before the mass-comparison trial. Travelling waves gain a wavelength ruler; the marked material point keeps its fixed horizontal coordinate. Resonance gains a damper, velocity-opposing direction guide and a steady-state response curve with the selected frequency marked.

Heat-exchange views add thermometers, contact detail and equal signed loss/gain values. A shared clock referenced to conductance 5 W/K makes conductance change the observed rate rather than merely rescaling animation time. Captured working substitutes current temperature, transferred heat and heat-transfer rate. Reverberation working likewise uses its captured shared clock. The temperature lesson holds amount/volume fixed and shows an isochoric pressure–temperature graph alongside kelvin/Celsius readings. Other gas lessons retain the isothermal volume graph. Thermal expansion shows a separate staged extension readout, stops added thermal traffic at zero temperature change, and preserves five decimal places in final metre length so a small contraction remains visible.
