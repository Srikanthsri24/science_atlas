Separatory funnel practical record

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited

Aim and hypothesis

Compare accurate, early and late switching. Record each liquid separately and distinguish mass purity from recovery.

Apparatus and model

Vented curved funnel, stopcock and two receivers. Two explicitly assumed immiscible phases; A density 1 g/mL. Original reference vessel, not a commercial calibration.

Procedure and observation instructions

  1. State the aim: investigate collection timing and density ordering for two assumed immiscible liquids.
  2. Record both starting volumes, B density, valve opening, discharge coefficient and collector-switch offset. A density is fixed at 1 g/mL.
  3. Keep the top vent open. Identify the lower phase; equal densities do not predict an ordering.
  4. Advance the reference time. Record interface and surface heights, pressure and flow. The curved vessel requires nonlinear volume calibration.
  5. Close at the interface for inspection. Record each component in both receivers and remaining in the funnel. Compare ideal, early and late switching using separate trials.
  6. Calculate receiver masses, target-component recovery and mass purity using the displayed substitutions. Check A, B and total-mass conservation.
  7. Write a conclusion supported by recorded numbers. State incomplete recovery when liquid remains and never describe an empty receiver as pure.

Precautions and limitations

Vented quasi-static orifice model with gravity 981 cm/s². No viscosity, emulsion, entrainment, capillarity, outlet holdup or airlock. Density does not establish immiscibility. Equal-density phase ordering uses the initial A-below-B convention. Collector switching is indexed to drained volume. Interface inspection pauses are excluded from the open-flow reference clock. Purity is a mass fraction, recovery refers to the initial target amount. Internal engineering review does not constitute independent expert certification.
