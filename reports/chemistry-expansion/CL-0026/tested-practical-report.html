Measure a liquid volume in a graduated cylinder

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs · Class 3 · CL-0026

Aim

Read the lowest water meniscus at eye level and distinguish viewing error from nearest-division rounding.

Apparatus and principle

Ideal 100 mL cylinder with 10–100 mL front graduations at 1 mL intervals, finite reservoir, metered transfer, adjustable observer and magnified eye view. The prescribed concave water surface conserves the delivered volume. Front marks are calibrated to its lowest central point. This is an ideal teaching calibration, not manufacturer certification.

Prediction

Changing eye height should change the apparent front-scale reading without adding or removing water. An eye level with the lowest meniscus should remove geometric parallax in this model.

Procedure

  1. Place the cylinder upright on the level model bench. Set a 50 mL target and horizontal eye distance 8 cm.
  2. Record the empty reservoir/cylinder evidence, then deliver the target over 20 model seconds. Pause before taking a reading.
  3. Keep the same water. Compare eye offsets +2 cm, −2 cm and 0 cm; follow the sight line to the front scale.
  4. Read the lowest meniscus point in the fixed eye-view inset. In this exercise round to the nearest 1 mL division.
  5. Record actual water, eye position, projected value, nearest-division reading and the separate errors. Write off scale instead of inventing an endpoint value.
  6. Repeat with another target or observer distance. Explain why half a division does not bound every source of measurement error.

Precautions and limitations

Read still water at the lowest meniscus point and keep the eye in its plane. The fixed eye-view follows the observer controls; orbiting the bench overview does not change the measurement. Front-scale projection assumes straight sight lines and thin glass, with refraction excluded. Meniscus curvature is prescribed, not calculated from a contact angle. Water transfer is accounted for immediately; streams are cues and tubing/in-flight inventory is not tracked. The exercise uses whole-division rounding; real glassware reading practices may include interpolation. Half-division resolution does not include calibration, temperature, drainage films or every error. Internal engineering review does not imply independent expert or official board certification.
