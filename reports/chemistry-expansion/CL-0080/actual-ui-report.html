CL-0080 · Construct an observed solubility curve

Aim,apparatus and procedure

Known masses,reference cooling chamber,thermometer and chosen finite-residue detector. This is a generic model,not calibrated real chemical data.

  1. Aim: construct an observed solubility curve using several known loadings and their first detectable clouding temperatures.
  2. Keep the reference material and water mass fixed. Prepare a known solute mass and begin with it completely dissolved at 70°C in this virtual reference.
  3. Cool at a controlled rate. Follow current temperature and the residue detector. The chosen model assumes immediate precipitation response without a nucleation barrier.
  4. Retain the temperature at the first detected event. The detector threshold is finite;this is not an exact measurement of the thermodynamic saturation temperature.
  5. Record water and solute balance readings,first-event thermometer reading,cooling rate,detection threshold and reporting increments. Earlier clear/transient observations may be retained but are excluded from the fitted curve.
  6. Repeat at five distinct loadings,for example20,28,36,44and52g/100gwater. Normalise each known initial solute mass by the water mass,not by total solution mass.
  7. Plot loading-based solubility estimate against observed cloud temperature. At least three distinct temperatures are required for the line calculation;five or more well-spaced trials are preferable.
  8. Calculate slope,intercept,residuals and the line estimate at25°C. Label extrapolation outside the observed temperature range. HighR² does not rule out a common thermometer offset or cooling/detector bias.
  9. Write a conclusion supported by the measurements. State that finite residue at detection and finite precipitation response make the clouding loading-ratio method approximate.

Prediction and plan

Collect five well-spaced loadings with one fixed protocol. Retain the first detected temperature and normalise weighed solute by water mass. Then compare rounding,bias and detector settings separately.
