Investigate a protective zinc coating

CL-0108 · Class 7 · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited

Aim and prediction

Compare matched controls before and after zinc exhaustion. <b>Prediction</b> is literal student writing.

Apparatus and principle

Separate bare, intact-zinc, contacted-scratch and detached-zinc reference cells distinguish barrier protection, limited sacrificial charge supply and an absent metallic path. Initial stocks, finite remaining metals and an explicit open material account are retained. Supplied currents are conditional teaching references, not measured corrosion kinetics.

Procedure and observation instructions

  1. State the aim and prediction; match iron mass, nominal zinc stock and carrier. Identify which zinc remains on a tray.
  2. Record the initial four controls. An intact coating is a barrier. Detached zinc has no metal path and leaves the iron exposed; both broad faces of detached zinc are wetted.
  3. Choose wet/dry and selected current budgets. These inputs define conditional references, not measurements of real corrosion rates. In dry controls the carrier is stored separately.
  4. Advance equal model time, pause, and record all controls together. Note zinc exhaustion, iron conversion and any unmet protective current.
  5. Separate protective electron-equivalent charge from zinc self-consumption. A bare-minus-control mass difference can also reflect barrier effects or oxygen competition; it alone does not establish cathodic protection.
  6. Use each phase’s actual duration and current for Q=IΔt. At a metal-capacity event the next phase uses new exposed areas and currents.
  7. Apply n=Q/(2F), then mass and external oxygen/water accounts. Hydroxide equivalents are bookkeeping, not a prediction of all real corrosion products.
  8. Write a result with values and units, then a conclusion limited to these assumptions. An actual gravimetric experiment requires defined cleaning, drying, blank correction and qualified supervision.

Result and conclusion

A barrier and protective metallic charge are different effects. Detached zinc can compete for oxygen but supplies no protective charge.

Assessment criteria

  • Correct matched stocks and four-control comparison.
  • Correct phase current, duration, charge, mass and units.
  • Separation of barrier effects, protective charge and oxygen competition.
  • Explicit open material account and appropriately limited conclusion.

Precautions and limitations

  • Selected demand, support and oxygen capacity are not calibrated polarization or weather data. No real coating lifetime is predicted.
  • Attached zinc shields its covered nominal iron area until it is exhausted. Detached zinc supplies no protective current, exposes all nominal iron area and has two wetted broad faces. Tiny holder contact footprints and edge reactions are omitted.
  • The shared oxygen capacity can make detached zinc compete for oxygen; this is distinct from metallic protective charge transfer.
  • Nominal areas remain fixed within a phase. Uniform iron loss and abrupt coating exhaustion are reference idealizations, not pit, crack or delamination predictions.
  • Only divalent Fe/Zn hydroxide-equivalent accounts are tracked. Unique mature rust composition, precipitation, pH, alloy layers, flaking and product volume are not predicted.
  • Carrier mass is maintained. Oxygen and net water are external inputs; dry carrier remains in a separate stock. This is not a sealed mass experiment.
  • Computational digits and tiny residuals are not measurement precision. Near-event traces have stock-scale floating-point uncertainty.
