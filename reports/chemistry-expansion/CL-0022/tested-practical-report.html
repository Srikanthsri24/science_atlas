Investigate dissolving sugar crystals

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs · CL-0022 · Class 2

Aim

Compare equal sugar masses in different numbers of grains and at different stirring settings, while conserving solid plus dissolved sugar.

Apparatus and principle

Water tray, equal cubic model grains in a permeable holder, stirrer and timer at a fixed reference 20 °C. Smaller grains give greater initial exposed area for equal mass. Dissolved sugar is invisible and remains in the liquid; clear water does not imply no sugar.

Prediction

With the same sugar mass, many smaller grains should dissolve faster because they expose more total area. Stirring should increase the chosen transfer rate. Dissolved sugar should remain in the liquid.

Procedure

  1. Start with 10 g sugar, 200 mL water, 8 coarse model grains and stirring 0.5.
  2. Record the initial state, then run and record at equal chosen times.
  3. Repeat with 512 smaller model grains while holding starting sugar mass, water and stirring fixed.
  4. Return to 8 grains and compare stirring 0 and 0.5.
  5. Check solid plus dissolved sugar and record any remaining solids at 120 s.
  6. Write a conclusion separating dissolution from sugar destruction and state a model limitation.

Result and conclusion

Greater initial exposed area increases the model dissolution rate at equal starting sugar mass. Stirring increases the chosen surface-transfer coefficient. Solid plus dissolved sugar remains equal to the starting mass: dissolution is not destruction. At 120 s, 10 g coarse grains without stirring retain 1.370962 g solid, while stirring 0.5 reaches full model dissolution. These cubic grains and chosen transfer rates are teaching approximations, not actual sucrose crystal habit or calibrated kitchen-sugar timings.

Precautions and limitations

Change one factor at a time. Model grains are equal cubes for geometry, not sucrose unit cells or actual crystal habit. A permeable holder makes all six faces effective exposed area; small support contacts are neglected. Bulk liquid is assumed well mixed even at zero stirring, which means background transfer rather than zero molecular motion. The chosen coefficient is not calibrated. Reference density, solubility and water density are approximations at 20 °C. Solution-volume changes, solid displacement, viscosity changes, fragmentation, clumping, evaporation and chemical reactions are omitted. Starting water mass/volume is distinct from final solution volume. The dissolved sugar is not visibly coloured; explanatory mass indicators are not molecular imaging. Do not taste laboratory samples. Internal checks do not imply independent expert or official board certification.
