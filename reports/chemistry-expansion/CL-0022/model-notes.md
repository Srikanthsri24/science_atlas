Fixed reference 20 °C. Equal cubic model grains, not actual sucrose habit/unit cells. Density 1.59 g/cm³. Water 200–400 mL, reference solvent density 1 g/mL; sugar 2–20 g; N in 8,64,216,512,1000. Reference capacity 2.04 g sugar/g starting water means every supported starting state is strongly undersaturated. Initial side a0 = [M0/(rho N)]^(1/3). Effective exposed area 6Na² assumes a permeable holder with negligible support contacts. Chosen surface coefficient k = 0.003 + 0.012 × stirring g/(cm² s). Shrinking-side equation da/dt = −2k/rho [1 − (M0 − rho N a³)/capacity]. Separated integral is evaluated analytically and inverted by bisection. The positive integral endpoint gives model completion time; after it, solid is exactly zero and dissolved sugar equals starting mass. All intermediate states conserve solid + dissolved sugar.

Independent fine-step RK4 comparisons agree with the analytic solution. Controls conserve sugar and solvent-plus-sugar mass, greater grain count increases initial area for equal mass, and stirring increases the chosen rate. Bulk liquid is assumed well mixed even at stirring zero; this is a lumped transfer model, not resolved diffusion or flow. Grain geometry changes as solid dissolves; dissolved sugar remains invisible in the liquid. Volume/displacement/viscosity changes, fragmentation, clumping, evaporation and chemical reactions are excluded. Composition is grams sugar per 100 grams starting water, not per final solution volume. The 120 s observation window may end with visible coarse grains; completion-time calculations are model predictions, not calibrated physical times.
