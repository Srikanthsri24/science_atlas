Observe water vapour condensing on a cool lid

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs · CL-0023 · Class 2

Aim

Compare droplet formation on lids at different maintained temperatures and conserve the sealed jar's water inventory.

Apparatus and principle

Sealed jar containing prepared humid air, temperature-controlled lid and bulk-air temperature, timer and prescribed droplet sites. Bulk liquid used during preparation has been removed before the model starts. Water vapour is invisible; the visible droplets are liquid water, not vapour.

Prediction

A sufficiently cool lid should collect liquid from water vapour already in the sealed jar. A warmer lid or lower initial humidity may prevent net condensation. Vapour plus droplets should keep the same total water mass.

Procedure

  1. Start with 1.5 L prepared air at 35 °C and initial humidity 80%.
  2. Choose a 10 °C maintained lid temperature. Record initial and 300 s states.
  3. Repeat with lids at 25 °C and 40 °C, holding initial air conditions fixed.
  4. Try initial humidity zero as a no-water-vapour control.
  5. Compare remaining vapour plus liquid droplets with initial water mass.
  6. Explain the source of the droplets, external heat removal and a model limitation.

Result and conclusion

The droplets come from water vapour initially inside the sealed jar. At the same initial air conditions, the 10 °C lid produces 0.034407373 g liquid by 300 s, while the 25 °C lid produces 0.013997520 g and the 40 °C control produces none. Remaining vapour plus liquid equals the initial inventory. Initial relative humidity differs from the current humidity as vapour is removed. Temperatures are externally maintained, and the transfer coefficient and fifty droplet sites are chosen teaching assumptions.

Precautions and limitations

The initial relative humidity is specified before condensation; remaining vapour changes during a trial. No new water enters the sealed jar, and no bulk liquid reservoir remains to evaporate. Bulk air and lid temperatures are externally maintained. The ideal-gas/empirical saturation model and chosen transfer coefficient do not resolve local air cooling, flow or calibrated condensation times. Fifty pinned ideal hemispherical sites are illustrative, not a nucleation prediction; gravity, coalescence and droplet shedding are excluded. Reference liquid density 1 g/cm³ and latent heat 2.45 MJ/kg are approximations. Warm-lid/no-vapour controls begin without droplets; evaporation of pre-existing droplets is outside scope. Internal checks do not imply independent expert or official board certification.
