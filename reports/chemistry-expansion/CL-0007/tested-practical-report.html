Find materials attracted to a magnet

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs · CL-0007

Aim

Compare six chosen specimen responses to an axial magnet at equal nominal gaps.

Apparatus and principle

Axial magnet model, six specimens, adjustable approach rail and ideal compliant pull sensor. The magnet field changes with distance. Material response and field gradient determine force; being a metal alone does not predict appreciable attraction.

Prediction

Iron and steel should show an appreciable pull; not every metal should do so.

Procedure

  1. Select iron, set a 25 mm nominal gap and record the magnet-off reference.
  2. Enable the magnet, let the staged approach finish, and record the sensor reading.
  3. Keep gap and pole unchanged; repeat for all six specimens.
  4. Compare iron at 25 and 100 mm, then reverse the pole.
  5. Describe the evidence, including below-threshold responses and a model limitation.

Result and conclusion

At the same 25 mm gap, the chosen iron and steel models show appreciable attraction. Aluminium and copper remain below the classroom threshold. Iron is weaker at 100 mm, and reversing polarity preserves attraction. These are illustrative reversible models, not measured material data.

Precautions and model limits

Keep nominal gap equal for fair material comparison. Report the numerical force and threshold; below threshold does not mean no magnetic field or exactly zero response. All parameters are original teaching examples, not calibrated specimen data. Point magnet/specimen, reversible induced moments and ideal spring equilibrium are assumed. Remanence, hysteresis, demagnetizing geometry, friction, impacts and dynamic vibration are excluded. Wood/plastic weak responses are neglected. Sensor displacement is magnified 100× in the diagram; geometry is schematic and numerical actual gap is authoritative. No real-device or material certification is implied.
