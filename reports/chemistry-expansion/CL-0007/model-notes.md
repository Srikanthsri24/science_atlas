# CL-0007 · Materials attracted to a magnet

Internal engineering model, not an independent scientific certification or official board mapping.

Six original illustrative specimens are attached to an ideal compliant pull sensor. A point magnet of moment 0.1 A m² approaches on its axis. B = polarity × 10⁻⁷ × 2m/r³. The reversible specimen moment is either a bounded tanh response (iron/steel) or a small linear susceptibility surrogate (aluminium/copper). Weak wood/plastic responses are neglected. Force toward the magnet is m_specimen(B) × 3B/r. Spring equilibrium solves kx = F(gap − x), k = 10 N/m, by bisection.

The nominal gap is 25–100 mm. Actual separation is nominal gap minus the calculated sensor shift. Sensor movement is displayed 100 times larger in schematic geometry; force and displacement readings use unmodified model outputs. Quasi-static approach changes are staged over four seconds. No impact, free-body launch, magnetic particles moving through field lines, or sensor vibration is claimed.

All specimen constants and the 1 µN classroom detection threshold are chosen teaching examples. They do not constitute material identification data. Hysteresis, remanence, specimen shape/demagnetization, friction, finite magnet geometry and dynamics are excluded. A below-threshold result does not imply no field or precisely zero force. Reversing polarity preserves attraction for the reversible iron model because both B and the induced moment reverse.

The 110-second guide compares all six samples. Practical trials compare equal nominal gaps, an off reference, reversed polarity and a farther separation. Reports include SI-unit substitution for field, moment, force and spring displacement, alongside student observations and conclusion.
