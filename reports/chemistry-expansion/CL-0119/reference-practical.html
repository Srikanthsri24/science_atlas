Solution conductivity practical

CL-0119 · Class8 · Science Atlas360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited

Aim and prediction

Compare selected references at matched geometry and distinguish RMS from waveform phase.

Apparatus and method

Virtual25°C reference solution, guarded parallel-plate cell, isolated low-voltage AC source and ideal in-phase bulk-current channel. No chemicals or electrical currents were physically prepared.

  1. State the aim and predict which reference adds mobile ionic carriers. Record the sample concentration as molality, temperature and assigned-reference type.
  2. Set the facing gap, plate width and immersed depth. Convert centimetres to metres and calculate the wetted facing area and ideal cell constant.
  3. Connect the low-voltage AC source and select its RMS voltage. The ideal in-phase channel excludes electrode-interface and reactive currents; it is not a raw impedance or real instrument-accuracy simulation.
  4. Distinguish RMS values from the current waveform phase. Positive and negative ions drift oppositely; external wire electrons move opposite conventional current. Particle counts and movement amplitude are schematic.
  5. Record the ideal RMS voltage/current and calculate conductance and reconstructed conductivity. Keep small water-background currents; do not round them to zero.
  6. Compare ideal water, ideal non-ionizing sucrose and a selected KCl molality with matched geometry and excitation. Change the gap or immersed area and test whether conductance changes while assigned conductivity stays fixed.
  7. Disconnect, lift the electrodes and set zero excitation as separate controls. Record reading unavailable, not zero solution conductivity, when the inverse measurement cannot be performed.
  8. Pause before recording a guided observation. Write result and conclusion with source-reference, electrode-model, time-magnification and water-safety limitations.
