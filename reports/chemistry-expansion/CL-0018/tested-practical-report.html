Mix coloured water and follow the colour

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs · CL-0018 · Class 2

Aim

Distinguish dilution from mixing while conserving a chosen tracer mass.

Apparatus and principle

Clear water, a coloured-water source with a known model tracer concentration, stirrer, timer and two imaginary sampling zones in one continuous liquid. Volumes are nominally additive; dye-volume and density changes are excluded. The model begins after the coloured source has been added. Mixing redistributes tracer between sampling regions; adding more water changes mean concentration.

Prediction

The same dye mass should have a lower mean concentration when more clear water is added. Stirring should reduce the difference between upper and lower samples.

Procedure

  1. Set 100 mL clear water, 25 mL coloured source and source concentration 0.1 mg/mL.
  2. Record the initial post-addition state, then stir and record at 120 model seconds.
  3. Repeat with 200 mL clear water while holding tracer mass constant; compare mean concentration.
  4. Repeat at stirring 0 and compare uniformity at equal time.
  5. Try zero coloured-source volume or zero source concentration. Do not assign a visible mixing result without tracer.
  6. Check top-plus-bottom tracer mass and explain the model's limitations.

Result and conclusion

Mixing redistributes conserved dye within the liquid; dilution lowers its mean concentration by increasing total volume. For 2.5 mg in 125 mL, mean concentration is 20 mg/L; in 225 mL it is 11.111111 mg/L. The two sampling regions are imaginary, and the exchange rate is a teaching assumption rather than calibrated fluid dynamics.

Precautions and limitations

The imaginary zones are not physical phases or separated containers. Their chosen exchange law is not a calibrated molecular-diffusion or fluid-flow prediction. Stirring changes the model exchange rate, while nominal volume and total tracer mass stay constant. Slow background exchange remains at stirring 0. No-tracer controls cannot demonstrate tracer redistribution. This model tracks tracer mass and nominal liquid volume, not solvent-density changes, evaporation, adsorption or chemical reactions. Colour shading is an explanatory concentration cue, not calibrated photometry. “Nearly uniform” requires visible tracer and contrast below 1%; finite-time concentrations need not be exactly equal. Do not taste laboratory samples. Internal engineering checks do not imply independent expert or official board certification.
