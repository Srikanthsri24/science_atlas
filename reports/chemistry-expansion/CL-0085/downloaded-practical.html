Filtration versus centrifugation

Aim,hypothesis and plan

Compare the methods at equal physical time. Change one condition at a time and retain separate water and per-size solid inventories.

Apparatus and reference preparation

Two equal 10 mL water samples,each containing 5 mg of each 1, 3 and 6 µm rigid sphere. Ideal capillary screen with collection receiver;balanced closed-lid rotor with a constant-area radial reference cell. A matched opposite cell balances the rotor;each notebook inventory concerns one cell.

Procedure and observation instructions

  1. Aim: compare the two methods for equal 10 mL water samples,each containing 5 mg of each 1, 3 and 6 µm reference sphere. State a prediction for each size.
  2. Record density,viscosity,screen opening,rotor setting,inner radius and hold duration. The balanced opposite rotor cell is a mechanical reference;the notebook accounts for one sample cell.
  3. Keep the rotor lid closed during acceleration,hold and deceleration. Use one elapsed physical clock for filtration and the speed program.
  4. Record actual rpm and RCF while running. At rest,actual RCF is zero;the peak setting and integrated squared-speed exposure remain relevant to the accumulated separation.
  5. Track filter upstream,captured and passed solids separately. A small captured fraction may reflect little liquid processed,not a failure to retain an encountered particle.
  6. Inspect the stopped cell. Record pellet and suspended mass for each size. All 10 mL water stays in the cell;no decanted product is assumed.
  7. Repeat one variable at a time. Calculate both method inventories,recovery fractions and solids per mL water. Do not equate clear appearance with dissolved-substance removal or safe water.
  8. Write a result supported by the recorded numbers and a conclusion restricted to these ideal models. Include the omitted effects and state when a size did not fully clear during the chosen program.

Result and conclusion

At the default 200 s endpoint, the filter captured 6.22945% of initial solid while the radial model pelleted 74.31872%. All 10 mL of water remained in the cell. Later filtration increased collected water without changing the stopped rotor pellet. These conclusions apply to the stated ideal models.

Precautions and limitations

The model uses incompressible dilute rigid spheres,water density 1000 kg/m³ and gravity 9.81 m/s². Filter porosity 0.4,path factor 5,thickness 0.0002 m,area 0.0005 m² are chosen references;real paper retention is not a geometric aperture. Feed is maintained well mixed;hydraulic head uses water volume in the dilute approximation. No blocking,depth capture or adsorption. Radial Stokes advection omits Brownian diffusion,hindered settling,secondary flow,transverse gravity settling,liquid counterflow and moving pellet-boundary corrections. Initial solid volume fraction is below 0.1%;maximum reference particle Re is below 0.05. Zero rpm means no outward centrifugal pellet,not no ordinary settling. The model does not decant liquid or predict dry-pellet purity. Rotor animation is slowed;physical calculations use actual rpm and integrated squared speed.
