Compare water and cooking oil pouring

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs · CL-0017 · Class 2

Aim

Compare two chosen reference liquids draining through matched narrow horizontal outlets under their falling reservoir heads.

Apparatus and principle

Separate constant-area reservoirs (20 cm²), matched primed tubes, collectors and model timer. Chosen 20 °C reference properties are water 1000 kg/m³ and 0.001 Pa·s, and oil 850 kg/m³ and 0.06 Pa·s. Real oils vary with composition and temperature. Quasi-steady laminar Poiseuille flow relates pressure, viscosity, radius and length; the reservoir head falls as liquid drains. This is a controlled narrow-outlet comparison, not an arbitrary tilted-beaker prediction.

Prediction

With matched outlets and equal starting volumes, the chosen water reference should drain faster than the more viscous oil reference.

Procedure

  1. Start both reservoirs at 100 mL with equal tube radius and length. Record at 0 model seconds.
  2. Compare remaining and collected volumes at equal model times.
  3. Repeat with radii 0.4, 0.6 and 0.8 mm while keeping other settings equal.
  4. Double tube length and compare the initial flow rates.
  5. Record both volume and tared liquid-mass balances. Distinguish nearly drained from exactly empty.
  6. Explain the chosen-reference comparison and a limitation involving real oil properties or surface tension.

Result and conclusion

Both liquids preserve their own starting volume and tared mass. At 100 mL each, radius 0.8 mm and length 20 cm after 3600 model seconds, water collected 99.999932016 mL while oil collected 18.224016704 mL. Head and flow decline together. Real oil properties and surface tension can change real drainage; the reference comparison is ideal.

Precautions and limitations

Use equal starting volume and matched geometry for comparisons. Both density and viscosity enter this chosen model, so differences are not attributed solely to viscosity. Flow is assumed fully developed, laminar and quasi-steady in primed horizontal tubes; all chosen controls remain below Re = 1000. Entrance losses, tube hold-up, outlet stream transit volume, capillary pinning, surface tension, menisci, evaporation and temperature change are excluded. The reservoirs and collectors contain separate liquids; masses are liquid-only (tared). A nearly drained flag does not set the remaining volume to zero. Real cooking oils and free pouring can differ substantially from these ideal reference cases. Do not taste laboratory samples. Internal engineering checks do not imply independent expert or official board certification.
