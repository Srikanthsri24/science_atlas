Recover sand by sedimentation and decantation

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · CL-0041 · Class 4 editorial investigation

Aim

Compare equal mixture withdrawals after different settling times. Account for wet sediment, remaining suspension and sand carried into the receiver.

Apparatus and model

Virtual source vessel, receiver and top-layer decanting adapter, 2 g monodisperse spherical quartz reference and 100 mL water. Radius 35–75 µm means diameter 70–150 µm. Corrected single-sphere drag determines terminal speed; uniform dilute suspension forms a saturated bed with solid fraction 0.6. Water density 1000 kg/m³, viscosity 0.001 Pa s, quartz density 2650 kg/m³ and g = 9.81 m/s² are reference approximations. Decantation is ideal top-layer withdrawal from a frozen settling snapshot, not a solved pouring flow.

Prediction

Waiting longer should form a clearer upper layer and reduce sand carried into an equal withdrawn mixture volume. The source residue will still contain water.

Procedure

  1. Prepare a fresh 2 g quartz / 100 mL water reference. Record the initial suspended and settled masses.
  2. Choose a settling time. Observe the clear upper layer and wet bed before removing any mixture.
  3. Withdraw a chosen top-mixture volume, up to 80 mL. Distinguish total mixture volume from water volume when sand is transferred.
  4. Repeat with the same radius and withdrawal volume after a longer wait. Change one variable at a time.
  5. Calculate receiver sand and water, source retained sand, wet residue and complete 102 g mass balance.
  6. Write a result and conclusion supported by the observations. State that retained wet residue is not dry recovered sand.

Result and conclusion

At 50 µm reference radius, compare the same 80 mL mixture withdrawal after 2 s and 20 s. Waiting longer reduces sand carryover in this model. Complete settling leaves 20 mL water with 2 g sand, giving 22 g wet source contents. Source plus receiver remains 102 g. Snapshot withdrawal freezes settling and omits resuspension and path hold-up.

Precautions and limitations

Use equal radius and withdrawn mixture volume for a fair waiting-time comparison. Do not treat staged transfer-animation seconds as physical flow duration. Settling is frozen during ideal withdrawal; path hold-up is neglected and no adapter hydrodynamics, sloshing, resuspension, tilt-driven flow, turbulence or continued sedimentation during pouring is solved. Reference spherical quartz is not a calibrated natural-sand size distribution. Startup acceleration, walls, hindered settling, flocculation and dissolution are omitted. Water in bed pores and above the bed remains in the wet source residue. Real clear liquid may contain dissolved substances or microbes. No measured purity, drinking-water safety or dry-sand yield is certified.
