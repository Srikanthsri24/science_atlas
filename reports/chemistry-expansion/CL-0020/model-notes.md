Each initial drop is a hemisphere at ideal contact angle 90°. V = 2πr³/3, and 1 µL = 1 mm³. Controlled motion closes an initial gap over a chosen 8 s. At contact, two tangent caps are replaced by one volume-conserving half-prolate-spheroid. Microscopic bridge growth is not resolved. Longitudinal axis a = R × stretch, transverse axes b = c = R/sqrt(stretch); hence 2πab²/3 = combined volume throughout relaxation. Chosen cubic easing reaches the joined hemisphere at 16 s. Timings are illustrative and not fluid dynamics.

Curved half-prolate-spheroid area = πb²[1 + a asin(e)/(b e)], e = sqrt(1 − b²/a²), with spherical limit 2πR². Surface energy = γA; area in mm² converts to m² before calculating joules. 0.07274 N/m is a reference near 20 °C. Initial/final hemisphere excess pressure is 2γ/r with r in metres. A stretched intermediate has nonuniform curvature, so no single equilibrium pressure is claimed. At ideal Young angle 90°, the net solid wetting-energy term vanishes; curved liquid–air energy is the reported quantity. No contact retains separate drops; hypothetical endpoint calculations are explicitly not observed joining.

Gravity, viscosity, pinning, contamination, evaporation and variable contact angle are excluded. Droplets are magnified. Small volumes 0.2–2 µL per starting drop limit size, but spherical shapes remain an approximation with gravity neglected.
