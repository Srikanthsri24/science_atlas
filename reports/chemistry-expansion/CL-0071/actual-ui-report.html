Separate a salt-and-sand mixture completely

CL-0071 · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · Virtual practical

Aim

Separate soluble reference salt from insoluble sand by dissolution, filtration, washing and drying. Test whether both products are completely separated, and account for water transferred into the air.

Principle and apparatus

Mixing beaker, stirring rod, ideal sand-retaining filter with a chosen mother-water retention, separate filtrate and residue dishes, reserved fresh wash water, virtual balance and process timeline. Salt dissolves; sand does not. Salt solution passes through the ideal filter, but retained solution can contaminate damp sand. Each fresh wash is perfectly mixed before drainage. Drying leaves salt from any retained solution. A rounded isothermal capacity reference0.36g salt/g water partitions dissolved and crystalline salt; all permitted initial preparations dissolve fully. No co-evaporation of salt or loss of sand is assumed.

Procedure

  1. Record original dry salt, sand and dissolving water. Count the reserved wash water inside the system boundary. Predict which product may remain contaminated.
  2. Run dissolution. Observe shrinking salt crystals and unchanged sand. Record the fully dissolved endpoint before filtration.
  3. Transfer the suspension through the ideal retaining filter. Record salt and water in both filtrate and damp residue. Do not identify filtrate as dry salt.
  4. Add a fresh wash, mix with retained mother liquor, then drain into the filtrate. Repeat the chosen number of washes and record each fill/drain endpoint.
  5. Dry filtrate and residue separately on the prescribed virtual schedule. Record salt crystals, sand, remaining liquid and water transferred to air. This schedule does not predict real drying time or heater power.
  6. Calculate salt recovery and dry sand-product purity. Repeat no wash, more washing and the explicit ideal zero-retention limit. Complete conservation checks and write the conclusion with limitations.

Prediction

Fresh washing should transfer retained salt into the filtrate. <balance> & purity must be checked separately.

Result and conclusion

Finite washing improves recovery; positive mother-water retention can leave salt with the dry sand. The zero-retention case is an ideal limit.

Report recovered dry salt in grams and percent, dry sand mass and salt contamination, and both product purities separately from wet masses. Finite washing with positive mother-water retention generally leaves some salt in the sand. Only the stated ideal zero-retention limit achieves perfect separation in this model. All original salt, sand and water remain accounted for, including invisible water transferred into the air.

Precautions and limitations

This is a virtual investigation. Real heating and drying require teacher supervision and suitable apparatus. The model assumes ideal retention of sand, no spills, instantaneous mixing within each wash, a fixed post-drain water hold-up and no adsorption or salt carried into air. Transfers and drying follow a chosen smooth schedule, not measured flow, dissolution, heat or drying kinetics. Exact dry endpoints are prescribed reference states, not a guarantee that real samples dry within1200seconds. Salt capacity, apparent solution volume0.3mL/g dissolved salt and solid packing0.6 are rounded/chosen visualization references, not calibrated composition/porosity measurements. Sand and salt are reference substances, not all natural sand or commercial mixtures. Editorial class placement; quantitative recovery formulas are teacher extensions.
