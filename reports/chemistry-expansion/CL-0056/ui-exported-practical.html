Observe a dissolving mass balance in a closed container

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · CL-0056 · Class 5 editorial investigation

Aim

Track salt in the cup, in transit, undissolved and dissolved. Compare changing compartments with unchanged whole-system mass.

Apparatus and model

Sealed outer vessel, internal salt cup and gate, water compartment, stirrer and reference balance. The outer lid stays closed. The 150 g apparatus tare includes fixed hardware and trapped air. The balance indication is static-equivalent: actual grain impacts, stirring forces and sensor transients are not simulated. In a real activity, allow the balance to settle before comparing readings.

Prediction

The sealed system should retain its whole mass even when visible salt decreases. Cup + flight + undissolved + dissolved must equal the initial salt. <check> & "units"

Procedure

  1. Record water, salt, chosen relaxation coefficient and apparatus tare. Include the salt in the internal cup before release.
  2. Start the internal 10 s release while keeping the outer lid closed. A chosen 0.1 s transfer delay means some salt is in flight before reaching the water.
  3. Pause at physical times before first arrival, during release, at 10 s, at 10.1 s and during continued dissolution. Record every salt compartment.
  4. Compare gross and net static-equivalent indications. Do not count only the receiving beaker or call dissolved salt lost matter.
  5. Repeat with different salt/water masses and relaxation coefficients. Compare finite-time residue with any capacity excess.
  6. Apply substitutions and check the salt and whole-material budgets. Explain why a reference volume change is not a mass change.
