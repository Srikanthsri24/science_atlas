Acidic and basic oxides with water

Science Atlas360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · CL-0100

Aim and prediction

Compare separate CO₂/water,CaO/water and pure-water stations. Distinguish gas partition,hydration and limited dissolution.

Compare separate stations <water>.

Apparatus and procedure

  1. Write the aim and predict how each oxide changes water. Keep the pure-water control separate.
  2. Record equal water volumes,the sealed chamber headspace,dry CO₂ stock at1atm25°C and initial weighed CaO. These are virtual,supervised stations.
  3. Meter a chosen gas fraction into the sealed chamber. Compare the calculated equilibrium pH with the water control. Record carbon retained in the syringe,headspace and water.
  4. Transfer a measured fraction of CaO from its pan to the independent basic station. Track the controlled hydration extent;never hydrate more oxide than was delivered.
  5. Record unreacted oxide,water consumed,dissolved calcium and undissolved hydroxide. Compare unsaturated and excess-solid preparations.
  6. Apply the formulas with your recorded values. State the observed evidence,calculated result,conclusion and model limitations separately.

Precautions and model limitations

  • Ideal quasistatic25°C model:concentration replaces activity;gas fugacity,ion pairing and real transfer kinetics are omitted. Calculated pH is not an experimental measurement.
  • CO₂* combines CO₂(aq) and H₂CO₃. The apparent first dissociation constant belongs to this combined pool. The model never identifies all dissolved carbon as carbonic acid.
  • The chamber is sealed with fixed nominal liquid and headspace volumes. Reported pressure is CO₂ partial pressure,not total pressure. Invariant inert carrier,water vapour,initial atmospheric CO₂ and apparatus tare are excluded from tracked oxide/water contents.
  • Each water preparation starts at nominal1g/mL. Liquid volume is subsequently held at its initial nominal value for equilibrium and drawing;liquid mass changes,so constant final liquid density is not asserted. Solid material adds displacement.
  • CaO hydration extent is a controlled teaching coordinate,not a measured time-dependent reaction rate. Each state equilibrates the aqueous hydroxide phase conditionally. Excess Ca(OH)₂ remains solid;CaO on the pan and unreacted CaO remain accounted.
  • Real CaO hydration releases heat. This model is held isothermally at25°C and does not predict temperature rise,steam or thermal burns. Physical CaO investigations require qualified supervision;this report describes the virtual investigation.
  • Solid markers retain equivalent material volume using nominal true densities3.34 and2.24g/mL. They are not real powder grain counts,bulk packing,crystal habit or calibrated cloudiness.
  • No colour illustration is a calibrated indicator spectrum or pH measurement. Numerical equilibrium detail is an optional advanced explanation.
