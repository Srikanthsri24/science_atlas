Water electrolysis: charge and gas-volume accounts

VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · CL-0121

Aim

Relate passed charge to ideal hydrogen and oxygen amounts and compare generated versus capacity-limited retained gas volumes.

Apparatus

Virtual regulated DC source, inert electrodes, non-chloride supporting electrolyte, separate graduated gas-volume accounts and timer.

Safety

Virtual investigation only; no real gases are generated. A real electrolysis practical requires teacher supervision, approved equipment, suitable ventilation and controlled chemicals. Never connect household mains to liquids or ignite collected gases.

Prediction and plan

Compare charge and gas amounts

Procedure

  1. State your prediction and identify negative cathode/H2 and positive anode/O2. The virtual cell assumes sufficient water with an inert non-chloride supporting electrolyte.
  2. Start a fresh comparison with stated current, gas temperature, dry-gas pressure and tube capacity. Changing temperature, pressure or capacity starts a fresh trial.
  3. Run a known interval. Record every current-duration-connection segment; disconnected intervals add elapsed time but no passed charge.
  4. Pause before recording. Read generated, retained and overflow volumes separately. The tube levels represent compensated dry-equivalent gas, not raw wet-gas meniscus readings.
  5. Apply Q=sum(It) over connected intervals, then nH2=Q/(2F) and nO2=Q/(4F). Convert temperature to kelvin and pressure to pascals before using V=nRT/P.
  6. Compare the generated volumes at common T/P. If a tube saturates, include its overflow; do not infer a new chemical ratio from capacity-limited retained volumes.
  7. Write an exam-style result with observations, substituted calculations, conclusion and limitations. Bubble-marker counts do not measure gas quantity.
