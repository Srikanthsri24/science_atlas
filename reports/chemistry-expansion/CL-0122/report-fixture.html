Copper electroplating: mass, charge and thickness

VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · CL-0122

Aim

Relate actual passed charge to copper transfer and average uniform film thickness, respecting a finite soluble copper anode.

Apparatus

Virtual regulated DC source, copper anode, masked conductive cathode, adequate well-mixed copper(II) electrolyte, timer, ideal mass readouts and magnified coating cross-section.

Safety

Virtual only; no real chemicals or currents are produced. Real electroplating requires teacher supervision, approved low-voltage equipment, suitable protective equipment and proper chemical-waste handling. Do not connect household mains to liquids.

Prediction and plan

Compare charge, finite inventory and coating thickness

Procedure

  1. Predict which electrode gains mass. Identify the positive copper anode and the negative masked conductive cathode in a copper(II) electrolyte.
  2. Start a fresh trial with independently stated initial masses and exposed width/height. Only one fully immersed face is exposed; other surfaces are masked.
  3. Record source-current, connection and duration segments. Source changes preserve earlier history; changes to initial masses or exposed geometry start a fresh comparison.
  4. Pause before recording. Note requested coulomb budget and actual passed charge separately. The ideal source opens when the finite copper anode is exhausted.
  5. Use Cu → Cu2+ +2e− at the anode and Cu2+ +2e− → Cu at the cathode. Apply m=QMCu/(2F), bounded by available anode copper.
  6. Record ideal final electrode masses and verify anode loss equals cathode gain. Net bath copper is unchanged in this ideal soluble-anode model.
  7. Calculate coating volume m/rho and average thickness V/A, retaining units. A magnified cross-section is a display aid; read its labelled scale.
  8. Write an exam-style result, substituted calculations, conclusion and limitations. These are ideal virtual readings, not a measured coating-quality or safety certification.
