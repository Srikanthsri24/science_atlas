Metals with dilute acid: finite stocks and hydrogen collection

VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · CL-0124

Aim

Compare selected metal/dilute-HCl references, limiting stocks and dry-equivalent hydrogen generation, retention and relief overflow.

Apparatus

Virtual reaction vessel, selected metal sample, defined dilute HCl, reference volume/mass inputs and ideal pressure-compensated gas collector with relief.

Safety

Virtual only. Real acid/metal practicals require teacher supervision, protective equipment and approved gas/waste handling. Hydrogen is flammable; no flame-test activity is provided here. Do not seal a real generating apparatus without approved relief.

Prediction and plan

Finite metal and acid; separate generated and retained hydrogen.

Procedure

  1. Select Zn, Fe or the Cu control with dilute non-oxidizing HCl. Predict the selected equation; do not generalize to nitric acid or other oxidizing conditions.
  2. Record initial metal mass, acid concentration and solution volume. Convert volume to litres and compare m/M with cV/2 to identify the limiting stock.
  3. Record collection temperature, pressure and capacity. Read the collector as an ideal dry-equivalent compensated volume, not a wet-gas measurement.
  4. Establish the contacted reference and advance controlled extent. Watch the original metal decrease and hydrogen collection increase; guide seconds do not measure reaction kinetics.
  5. Pause and record original metal remaining, acid consumed/remaining, metal ions and generated hydrogen. Keep retained and relief-overflow gas separate.
  6. Repeat with Cu, no contact, no metal and no acid controls. Change one starting condition per fresh comparison; rewinding the reference does not chemically reverse a reaction.
  7. Substitute values into n=m/M, n=cV and V=nRT/P with kelvin and pascal units. Account for two acid equivalents and two formal electrons per hydrogen molecule.
  8. Check metal, acid-hydrogen and chlorine atom accounts, and chloride-balanced solution charge. State the result and model limitations in an exam-style conclusion.
