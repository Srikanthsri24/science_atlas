Plan a separation sequence for a three-component mixture

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · CL-0054 · Class 4 editorial investigation

Aim

Plan and test a dry-first route to separate iron, sand and salt. Check product purity, recovery and each component mass.

Apparatus and materials

Covered magnet, iron collection dish, mixing beaker, stirrer, reserved water, ordinary particle filter, funnel, receiver/evaporation dish and labelled product containers. Model reference tares: iron collector 12 g, filter 2 g, dish 30 g. Readings are dry-equivalent where labelled; real wet-paper masses require drying and tare subtraction.

Prediction

I predict that the dry-first route will collect iron with a magnet, dissolve salt in enough water, retain sand during filtration and recover salt crystals after evaporation. Too little water should leave some salt mixed with the sand.

Procedure

  1. Record starting iron, sand, salt and reserved water masses. Include the reserved water in the whole-system ledger before adding it.
  2. Plan the dry-first order: magnet, add water/stir, filter, evaporate filtrate. This is a controlled teaching route; different handling can permit other experimental orders.
  3. Collect the iron selectively. Add the reserved water to the remaining sand/salt and mix. The chosen 25 °C salt capacity is 0.36 g per gram water.
  4. Filter the undissolved solids. If water was insufficient, some undissolved salt remains mixed with the sand; do not call the residue pure sand.
  5. Follow the evaporation time-lapse. Water transferred to air stays in the mass budget. Compare dissolved salt with salt crystals.
  6. Pause at stage boundaries andrecord the table. Procedure progress 0–4 is prescribed animation progress,not elapsed physical seconds or measured kinetics.
  7. Repeat with insufficient water. Apply the formula substitutions and distinguish salt crystal mass from wet dish contents.
