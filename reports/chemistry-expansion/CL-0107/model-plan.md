Use matched iron specimens and explicitly defined sodium-chloride solution preparations. Distinguish mass percent of final solution from grams per100g of water, and from practical salinity. ## Candidate quantitative design for implementation

The strongest candidate is a controlled transport/current-ceiling reference, not an invented empirical rust-speed curve. Retain the full concentration-comparison experience through two solution modes: a restricted dilute-ion approximation, and a general entered-conductivity/density mode for broader salt concentrations. Clearly label entered reference values; do not imply they were measured unless supplied as actual observations. Dissolved oxygen is an independently specified controlled value, not falsely derived from NaCl percentage with a seawater-salinity equation.

Dilute mode can use a selected limiting NaCl molar conductivity of126.5 S cm2/mol =0.01265 S m2/mol from the primary Mainz laboratory course. Its concentration approximation must have an explicit restricted domain and neglect finite-concentration interactions transparently. Broader solutions require supplied conductivity and density; do not extrapolate the limiting value to concentrated brine. Water has a nonzero background conductivity. The concentration definition is NaCl mass/final solution mass; derive water mass, amount, volume using the selected density, molarity and molality separately.

Use matched edge-masked flat iron coupons with exposed planar area A across two faces. Side width is sqrt(A/2), and thickness follows the iron material volume. This gives a physically meaningful constant exposed area while thickness decreases, until metal exhaustion. Do not silently change exposed area or invent rust expansion. Show the qualitative surface separately from metal mass/thickness and distinguish cleaned metal loss from retained-product mass gain.

For a provisional electrochemical account: primary Fe -> Fe(II) +2electrons gives n_Fe = Q_primary/(2F). Oxygen reduction consumes n_Fe/2mol O2; assumed rapid maturation to an Fe(III) product needs a further n_Fe/4mol O2. Total oxygen demand is3n_Fe/4. If both steps share the same steady oxygen-transport supply J=D*C/delta, the compatible primary-current ceiling is j_O2=(8/3)*F*J, not4*F*J. The latter would omit oxygen used in further oxidation. The Ohmic ceiling j_Ohm=conductivity*effective electrolyte drop/path length is only a resistance bound after separately assumed electrochemical driving; it is not a measured corrosion current or a full applied-voltage/electrode-kinetics model. A selected fraction of min(j_Ohm,j_O2) can define a clearly authored reference scenario. This must not be called an experimentally validated corrosion-rate law.

Bulk concentration and oxygen may be maintained by an explicit ideal replenishment reservoir. If used, track cumulative oxygen and hydration-water supplied and include those terms in the material account. Do not use a sealed-system conservation equation while adding external oxygen. Actual charge must stop at metal exhaustion; separate initial ceiling, current after exhaustion, time-averaged current and mass-loss rate. A zero-current control must not generate product. Any further mechanistic omissions, hydration assumptions and parameter support must be documented before implementation.

Validation should independently derive SI conversions, dual oxygen demand, charge/mass/atom closure, finite metal exhaustion, zero controls, equal-solution symmetry, water-background conduction, oxygen-limited plateaus, changes in supplied oxygen, and the distinction between inferred dilute conductivity and entered values. The dual-mode interface and scope remain design decisions, not implemented features yet.


## Implemented core decisions

The source model now has dilute and entered-property modes. Dilute mode is explicitly restricted to0-0.05mass-percent NaCl, with selected limiting conductivity0.01265 S m2/mol, selected water-background conductivity5.5e-6 S/m and an ideal density1g/mL. Entered mode supports0-10mass-percent with supplied total conductivity and density. The model does not certify empirical consistency of user-entered property values. Nominal oxygen diffusivity2e-9m2/s is an authored reference coefficient, not a fitted salt-concentration law. Bulk dissolved oxygen remains an independently specified boundary condition.

The effective electrolyte drop is a selected resistance-driving assumption, not the full applied cell voltage. Activation polarization, separate anode/cathode kinetics, chloride-specific surface chemistry and evolving oxide barriers are omitted. The same selected nominal exposed area is used as the effective transport/conduction area. The resulting min(Ohmic,O2-transport) current and chosen fraction are conditional reference quantities, not measured corrosion data.

The carrier bath is maintained at its prepared composition/density. Net hydration water and oxygen entering the retained product are explicitly replenished and included in the open-account balance. Constant trace dissolved-oxygen inventory, apparatus and unchanged inert gases are outside the incremental carrier-account boundary. FeII intermediates are assumed to mature immediately to the selected Fe2O3.H2O equivalent; no intermediate inventory or universal rust composition is inferred.

The geometry uses horizontal edge-masked square coupons. Both faces fit under the prepared carrier envelope at every supported mass, area, density and volume corner. The initial-level reference does not infer a variable rust density or evolving rust-product displacement. Cumulative-loss histories include finite-metal exhaustion knots; current stops at exhaustion. Penetration is calculated directly from converted Fe volume rather than by subtracting near-equal thicknesses, preserving supported trace values.
