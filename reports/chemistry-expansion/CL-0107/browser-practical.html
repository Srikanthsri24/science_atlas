Compare saltwater concentration and corrosion references

CL-0107 · Class 7 · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited

Aim and prediction

Compare salt references while matching oxygen and geometry. <b>Prediction</b> stays student text.

Apparatus and principle

Two virtual maintained carrier baths and matched edge-masked iron coupons compare solution properties, oxygen supply and a selected current-ceiling fraction. Ionic resistance and oxygen transport are separate limits. Cleaned-metal loss and retained-product gain are different quantities. These are chosen reference scenarios, not measured corrosion-rate data.

Key terms and interpretation

  • Mass percent compares salt mass with the final solution mass. Molarity uses solution volume; molality uses solvent mass. They are different concentration definitions.
  • Conductivity describes how readily the carrier supports ionic charge movement. Resistance also depends on the selected path length and area.
  • A current ceiling is a conditional limit within the stated model. The selected fraction sets a reference scenario; it is not a measured corrosion efficiency.
  • The Faraday constant is charge per mole of electron equivalents. The primary FeII account uses two per converted Fe; further FeIII maturation is tracked separately.
  • Cleaned-metal loss counts iron converted. A retained specimen can gain mass because oxygen and hydration water enter its product account.
  • An open material balance includes external material supplied. A maintained bath must not be analysed as though it were a sealed container.

Procedure and observation instructions

  1. Write the aim and prediction. Compare salt concentration while matching initial iron mass, prepared exposed two-face area and carrier-solution mass. State whether oxygen and other reference settings are also matched.
  2. Choose dilute or entered-property mode. Dilute mode uses a restricted leading-order conductivity approximation and selected density. Broader concentrations require supplied conductivity and density. Entered reference values are not automatically verified measurements.
  3. Record all inputs before advancing time. Calculate salt mass from percent of the final solution mass, then water mass by difference. Do not use grams per 100 g of water as though it were final-solution mass percent.
  4. Record the initial matched observation. The model uses ideal maintained baths: carrier composition and bulk oxygen are held constant by replenishment. This is not a sealed test-tube mass account.
  5. Advance the same illustrative model time for both coupons and record both cells together. Distinguish resistance and oxygen ceilings from the selected fraction of that ceiling. A changed oxygen input is an additional variable, so the outcome cannot be attributed to salt alone.
  6. Record primary charge, cleaned-metal loss, retained-product gain and externally supplied oxygen/water. The extra oxygen for assumed FeII-to-FeIII maturation is included separately from primary cathodic reduction.
  7. Repeat with equal solutions, zero driving drop, zero selected fraction or an oxygen-limited comparison. At metal exhaustion, current and further charge accumulation stop; the time average can remain positive.
  8. Show formula, substituted values, result and units. Write a conclusion limited to the chosen reference assumptions. A physical cleaned-metal-loss experiment needs a defined cleaning/drying procedure and blank correction; weighing a wet or rust-coated specimen does not directly give cleaned-metal loss.
