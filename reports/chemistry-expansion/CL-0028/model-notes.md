# CL-0028 — How stirring changes dissolving time

Internally reviewed 2026-11-25. Both samples have 10 g sugar, 200 g starting water (reference 200 mL), 64 equal cubic grains and reference temperature 20 °C. This is a controlled paired investigation using the unchanged, already reviewed CL-0022 shrinking-grain primitive. The original inventory and completed lab are preserved.

Only normalized stirring settings differ. They change a chosen surface-transfer coefficient k = 0.003 + 0.012u g/(cm² s). No RPM, liquid-flow solver or empirically calibrated time is claimed. The bulk is assumed well mixed even at u = 0, so this is a lumped dissolution comparison, not resolved diffusion. Solubility and density assumptions/provenance remain in CL-0022/model-notes.md.

Time to 90% dissolved is the time to 9 g dissolved in each sample. A bisection at u = 0 establishes the baseline threshold; for identical initial conditions the separable equation makes time scale inversely with k. The target is validated by independently substituting the resulting time into the shrinking-grain state. A threshold is only marked reached when the current model time has reached it. Average rate is dissolved mass divided by elapsed time and is undefined at time zero. Once the faster sample has dissolved, its instantaneous rate is zero; it need not exceed the slower sample's instantaneous rate at every later time.
