Find how stirring changes dissolving time

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs · Class 3 · CL-0028

Aim

Compare two stirring settings while keeping the sugar mass, water, grain count and reference temperature the same. Compare model times to 9 g dissolved, rather than different endpoints.

Apparatus and principle

Two matched model dissolution cells with 200 mL starting water, 10 g sugar in 64 supported equal cubic grains, separate stirring controls and one clock. Reference temperature is 20 °C. Stirring changes a chosen surface-transfer coefficient. Dissolved sugar remains in the liquid, so solid plus dissolved sugar equals 10 g in each cell. Stirring settings and model times are not calibrated RPM or physical stopwatch measurements.

Prediction

QA trial: with matched sugar, water, grains and temperature, cell B at setting 1 should reach 9 g dissolved before cell A at setting 0.

Procedure

  1. Set A to 0 and B to 1. Verify the identical starting quantities before the release.
  2. Run both together. Pause at the same model times, for example 0, 10, 30, 60 and 120 s, and record both solid and dissolved masses.
  3. Compare the actual state with the 9 g target. Before it is reached, write not reached; do not invent an observed crossing.
  4. Compare the two predicted model crossing times and calculate t_A/t_B. Keep this dimensionless ratio separate from mean rate in g/s.
  5. Repeat with equal settings as a control, then exchange A and B. Changing a setting resets both cells for a new comparison.
  6. Write a result supported by the records and explain a limitation of the chosen kinetic model.

Precautions and limitations

Change only the intended stirring setting, reset both samples together and compare the same elapsed time or dissolved fraction. Sugar is invisible once dissolved. The model assumes well-mixed bulk solution even at stirring zero, equal cubic grains, constant reference temperature and a prescribed transfer coefficient. Rod motion indicates the selected setting; it does not solve fluid flow or calibrate RPM. Grains are held on a permeable support so the rod does not collide with them. Contact area, breaking, clumping, evaporation, reaction, displacement and solution-volume changes are excluded. Water level shows starting solvent only. All recorded values are simulated evidence.
