Compare water absorption by paper, cloth and plastic

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs · CL-0004

Aim

Compare retained water and mass gain in three specified virtual samples under equal conditions.

Apparatus

Balance, dry samples, water bath, timer and a consistent draining procedure.

Principle

Retained water adds mass to a sample. Compare dry and wet masses after the same soaking and draining protocol. Absorption is distinct from water passing through a hole.

Prediction

Equal dry samples may retain different amounts of water. I predict paper will show the largest mass gain in these chosen models.

Procedure

  1. Select equal dry masses and equal bath water amounts.
  2. Weigh each dry sample.
  3. Soak for the same modelled interval.
  4. Lift and drain for the same short interval.
  5. Weigh after draining, record both masses and compare.

Teacher extension: formulas and substitution

W = min(V₀, mC[1 − exp(−kt)]). V₀ is available water (mL), m is dry mass (g), C is specific capacity (mL/g), k is a rate constant (s⁻¹) and t is soak time (s). Water density is approximated as 1 g/mL.

Trial 1: Capacity = dry mass × specific capacity = 2.00 × 3 = 6.000 mL. Retained water W = min(20.00, 6.000 × [1 − exp(−0.06 × 60.0)]) = 5.836 mL. With ρ = 1 g/mL, wet mass = 2.000 + 1 × 5.836 = 7.836 g. Mass gain = 5.836 g. Uptake = 100 × 5.836 / 2.000 = 291.80%. Values are calculated before rounding.

Trial 2: Capacity = dry mass × specific capacity = 2.00 × 2 = 4.000 mL. Retained water W = min(20.00, 4.000 × [1 − exp(−0.025 × 60.0)]) = 3.107 mL. With ρ = 1 g/mL, wet mass = 2.000 + 1 × 3.107 = 5.107 g. Mass gain = 3.107 g. Uptake = 100 × 3.107 / 2.000 = 155.37%. Values are calculated before rounding.

Trial 3: Capacity = dry mass × specific capacity = 2.00 × 0 = 0.000 mL. Retained water W = min(20.00, 0.000 × [1 − exp(−0 × 60.0)]) = 0.000 mL. With ρ = 1 g/mL, wet mass = 2.000 + 1 × 0.000 = 2.000 g. Mass gain = 0.000 g. Uptake = 100 × 0.000 / 2.000 = 0.00%. Values are calculated before rounding.

Trial 4: Capacity = dry mass × specific capacity = 0.50 × 3 = 1.500 mL. Retained water W = min(0.00, 1.500 × [1 − exp(−0.06 × 15.0)]) = 0.000 mL. With ρ = 1 g/mL, wet mass = 0.500 + 1 × 0.000 = 0.500 g. Mass gain = 0.000 g. Uptake = 100 × 0.000 / 0.500 = 0.00%. Values are calculated before rounding.

Result and conclusion

At 2 g dry mass, 20 mL water and 60 seconds, the chosen paper model retained more water than cloth; the chosen plastic model gained no mass. In an empty-bath control, 0.500 g paper stayed at 0.500 g. These are illustrative samples, with loose surface drops excluded.

Precautions and limitations

Use equal masses, water amounts, soak times and drainage. Weigh after lifting and draining, never while immersed. Coefficients are chosen teaching parameters, not measured universal material properties. No swelling, evaporation, dissolution or loose surface-droplet model is included. Plastic is assigned zero internal uptake for this chosen short test; that is not a universal statement about all polymers. The guide uses staged time. A report export does not certify the student's conclusion.
