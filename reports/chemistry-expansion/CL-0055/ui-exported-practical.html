Distinguish reversible and irreversible material changes

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · CL-0055 · Class 5 editorial investigation

Aim

Compare three changes and test whether a specified classroom operation restores the material or its original connections. Distinguish physical/chemical classification from easy classroom reversibility.

Apparatus and model

Reference ice/water vessel with controlled latent-energy reservoir; paper sheet and straight cutter; virtual gas-tight reaction vessel and frictionless constant-pressure dry-gas collector. Read the assumptions below. The collector is inside the mass boundary; this is not a rigid sealed-container experiment.

Prediction

I predict that removing the same latent energy will restore the ice phase. Cut paper should remain paper but keep its seams after alignment. Stopping the bicarbonate/acetic acid reaction should not regenerate the original reactants; collected gas must remain in the mass budget.

Procedure

  1. Choose a sample and record the relevant starting conditions. Predict the change and whether the specified return operation will restore it.
  2. For ice, supply then remove equal latent energy at the 0 °C reference phase boundary. Record phase masses, energy and volumes.
  3. For paper, make the prescribed straight cuts. Record full cuts, partial cut and pieces. Bring the fragments back to alignment without adhesive and inspect the seams.
  4. For the chemical reaction, follow bicarbonate and acetic acid conversion. Stop stirring and return the tool. Record products and the ideal dry gas equivalent, including collector gas in total mass.
  5. Pause before recording. Progress 0–2 is prescribed procedure progress, not physical seconds or a measured reaction/heating rate. The guide gives each experiment 40 of its 120 presentation seconds.
  6. Apply case-specific calculations. Compare the evidence and explain why cut paper can be a physical change without being restored by simple alignment.

Result, conclusion and limitations

The 50 g ice cycle exchanged 16700 J in each direction and returned to 50 g solid water at the reference phase boundary. Three complete cuts produced four paper pieces; closing the gaps did not reconnect the sheet. With 2 g bicarbonate and 100 g acid solution, condensed material plus collected CO2 remained 102 g. The operation tested, material identity and system boundary determine the conclusion; physical change does not always mean simple classroom reversibility.
