Does this reference jacket slow cooling?

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited

Aim

To compare matched bare and jacketed cooling vessels and reconcile stored energy with side and other-path heat loss.

Student hypothesis and plan

Keep water mass and initial temperature equal. Compare common elapsed times. Change one condition and reconcile both heat-loss paths. <safe plan>

Apparatus and model preparation

Matched well-mixed water/vessel references with water radius 0.035 m, thin wall outer radius 0.036 m and rim height 0.16 m. Water inventory fixes wetted height. Room 25°C. Generic jacket conductivity,constant outside heat-transfer coefficient and selected parallel lid/bottom conductance. The jacket has no heat storage in this quasi-steady model.

Procedure and observations

  1. Aim: compare the cooling of matched bare and jacketed vessels,while accounting for side and lid/bottom heat losses.
  2. Record equal water masses,initial temperature,vessel heat capacity and room temperature. Record jacket thickness and chosen conductivity,outside heat-transfer coefficient and the separate other-path conductance. These are reference settings,not calibration of named products.
  3. Start both vessels at the same physical time. Record temperatures at equal intervals such as 0, 600, 1200 and 1800 s. The 120-second presentation clock is different from the physical cooling clock.
  4. Record water/vessel and outer-surface temperatures separately. The model jacket has no thermal storage;its surface follows a quasi-steady resistance calculation rather than a resolved initial warming front.
  5. Calculate wetted height from water volume. Use the cylindrical side area and logarithmic jacket resistance,not the flat-wall thickness/area formula. Thermal resistances here are whole-vessel K/W,not a manufacturer R-value per unit area.
  6. Keep the side path in series with outside convection,and add the chosen lid/bottom path in parallel. Calculate each time constant and predicted temperature.
  7. Reconcile water/vessel stored energy with total heat released to the room. Retain side and other-path partitions and the numerical residuals.
  8. Compare initial watts,current watts and accumulated joules separately. The warmer jacketed sample can eventually lose more watts while still having lost fewer total joules. A conductive cover can increase loss by increasing outside area.
  9. Repeat while changing one setting. Include zero jacket thickness and equal initial/room temperature controls. Write a conclusion limited to this constant-property,well-mixed,massless-jacket model.
