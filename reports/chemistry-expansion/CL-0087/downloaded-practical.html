Pure-substance and mixture melting

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited

Aim,hypothesis and plan

Compare equal amounts and net heat rates. Record each phase and test whether the eutectic is a counterexample to a purity-only conclusion.

Apparatus and reference system

Paired virtual sample cells with independent ideal net-heat controllers,a common elapsed clock,phase inventories and temperature traces. A and B are hypothetical teaching substances,not a procedure for handling real chemicals.

Procedure and observation instructions

  1. Aim: compare the onset and completion of melting in pure reference A and a selected composition of hypothetical A/B substances. Predict whether changing composition changes the melting range.
  2. Record A mole fraction,amount in each sample and net heat rate delivered to each sample. Both samples begin at 5°C. A and B are teaching references,not named chemicals or measured apparatus data.
  3. Run the shared physical clock. Observe the first liquid,the last solid and the two temperature curves. During the guided animation,the presentation clock is different from the displayed physical clock.
  4. Capture an initial row,a row during the eutectic or pure melting plateau,a row while an excess solid remains if present,and a row at completion. Record the physical time in each row.
  5. Track solid A,liquid A,solid B and liquid B separately. Add each component across its phases before calculating the total;visual changes do not create or destroy material.
  6. Repeat with an A-rich mixture,a B-rich mixture and the exact eutectic preset. Change one preparation variable at a time;changing preparation starts a fresh run.
  7. Use Q = Pnet t only before the controller cutoff. Afterwards subtract blocked heat. Calculate sensible and component latent heat separately,then check that they sum to delivered heat.
  8. Write the result as onset,completion,range and phase amounts with units. Explain the eutectic counterexample: a mixture can also melt sharply. Restrict the conclusion to ideal equilibrium assumptions.
