Filter insoluble model powder from water

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · Class 3 · CL-0033

Aim

Compare captured and passed powder, collected water and changing flow using a fine ideal opening, a coarser opening and a no-cake control.

Apparatus and principle

Upper suspension cell, ideal filter, support stand, funnel outlet, receiver and clock. Begin with100 mL reference water and2 g insoluble 20 µm model particles. Continuous ideal mixing is assumed. A chosen declining-reference-head/series-resistance law sets flow; nominal opening size is an ideal cutoff, not real paper efficiency.

Prediction

QA: a5µm ideal opening should retain20µm particles, while a50µm opening should pass them. With the same chosen clean conductance, captured material should increase cake resistance and reduce collection at the same time.

Procedure

  1. Prepare100 mL reference water and2 g powder. Set opening5 µm and cake factor1g⁻¹, then record zero time.
  2. Start and pause at matching model times. Record upstream and collected water, upstream powder, captured powder and powder in the receiver.
  3. Check water adds to100 mL and powder to2 g. Finite-time residue remains upstream.
  4. Inspect a single ideal opening. Compare its size with20 µm particles; this encounter is explanatory, not a measured fluid trajectory.
  5. Prepare a fresh coarse-opening control and compare the same time. Repeat with no cake resistance and with a larger cake factor.
  6. Substitute values into the integrated flow and mass-balance formulas. Write a supported result, conclusion and limitations.
