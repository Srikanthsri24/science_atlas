# CL-0033 — Filter insoluble model powder from water

Original ideal filter model, not measured filter-paper performance. Start with2g monodisperse20µm insoluble model powder and100mL reference water, approximated as100g. Continuous ideal mixing prevents the sedimentation explored inCL-0032. The ideal opening cutoff retains particles only when diameter is greater than selected pore opening2–50µm; real paper efficiency is not a sharp size switch. A coarse-opening control passes powder into filtrate. No adsorption, depth capture, flocculation, pore-size distribution or chemical reaction is modeled.

Let x=remaining water/100mL. A chosen declining-head/series-resistance law is q=2x/(1+β×captured powder g)mL/s, β=0–2g⁻¹. Initial chosen flow is2mL/s. Captured powder=η×2(1−x), upstream=2x, filtrate powder=(1−η)×2(1−x), where ideal retentionη is0 or1. Then dx/dt=−0.02x/[1+b(1−x)], b=2βη. Exact integral: (1+b)ln(1/x)−b(1−x)=0.02t, inverted by bounded bisection. With b=0 the independent closed solution is x=exp(−0.02t). Positive finite-time upstream residue is retained through600s. No exact-drained claim is allowed.

Water and powder are conserved separately across upstream, filter and receiver. Reference water density1g/mL gives total102g. Solid displacement, filter-water storage, evaporation, leakage and detailed pressure/flow/cake structure are excluded; these limitations must be visible in the future UI/report. This is a chosen quasi-static resistance model, not a calibrated Darcy coefficient or quantitative real filter efficiency. Its defensibility comes from dimensional consistency, stated assumptions, conservation, monotonic controls and independent numerical validation.
