Choose a material for a reusable drinking cup

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs · CL-0012

Aim

Use controlled water-holding and empty-drop evidence to compare chosen material scenarios.

Apparatus and principle

Matched cup shapes, 200 mL water, leak collector and timer; separate identical dry specimens, adjustable release height and impact floor. Cup mass depends on chosen density. Water leakage follows a head-conductance model; drop energy follows E = m_empty gH.

Prediction

A material should hold water and withstand the chosen empty-cup drop. Glass may hold water but fail a higher drop; more than one material can meet the chosen criteria.

Procedure

  1. Select a material and hold 200 mL for 60 model seconds. Record retained water and caught loss.
  2. Switch to a separate dry specimen and drop it empty from 0.30 m. Record the outcome after impact.
  3. Repeat both tests for every material at equal geometry and conditions.
  4. Try a lower glass drop height, an incomplete test and a zero-fill control.
  5. Choose a material for the stated criteria and justify it with recorded evidence.

My material choice

Polymer model

Result and conclusion

For the chosen 200 mL/60 s and 0.30 m empty-drop criteria, polymer and steel meet both tests. I choose polymer when lower dry mass matters for matched dimensions. Glass holds water but fails the 0.30 m scenario; it meets the gentler 0.05 m scenario. Paper loses water. Incomplete and zero controls cannot establish a completed comparison. These illustrative thresholds do not certify real drinking-cup safety or environmental superiority.

Precautions and limitations

Use separate matched specimens for the water and empty-drop tests. Do not infer a completed comparison from an incomplete or zero-fill control. Conductances, densities and damage thresholds are illustrative scenario parameters, not calibrated specimens or real fracture predictions. The drop stops inelastically at the floor; rotation, bounce, impact force, fragment trajectories and contact-dependent fracture are not solved. A damage marker illustrates the threshold flag. The leakage model assumes fixed geometry and ignores wetting delay, swelling, collapse and evaporation. No drinking-water, food-contact, hygiene or life-cycle sustainability certification is implied. Multiple materials may meet the chosen criteria; a different drop height can change the conclusion. Recorded times are model times; manual water playback is compressed 6× and drop playback is slowed 6×.
