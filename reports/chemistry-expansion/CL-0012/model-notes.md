# CL-0012 · Choosing a reusable cup — model under development

The two-experiment 2D/3D workbench and practical record have passed internal functional review. Evidence is recorded in review.json; no independent expert or official-board certification is claimed.

Matched ideal specimens have internal radius 3 cm, internal height 8 cm, 2 mm walls and a 2 mm base. Capacity is 72π mL. Dry cup mass follows a chosen material density and the difference between outer and inner cylinder volumes. The paper, polymer, glass and steel labels describe illustrative scenarios, not calibrated specimen measurements.

Two separate identical specimens are used: an undropped cup for a 200 mL, 60 s leak test, and an empty dry cup for a controlled-height drop. The leakage model is V(t) = V0 exp[−(k/A)t], where A is internal area and k is an illustrative head conductance. Only the uncoated paper example has nonzero conductance. Retained plus caught water is conserved. Impermeable examples remain leak-free within this ideal model.

The empty-cup drop uses h_fallen = min(H, ½gt²) and impact energy E = m_empty gH. Motion stops inelastically at the floor. A scenario-specific chosen damage threshold produces a damage flag; no fracture mechanics, impact force or real material safety rating is predicted. Heights range from zero to 1 m. Damage is not declared before impact.

The selection flag requires a completed 200 mL/60 s leak test with at most 1 mL loss, an actual completed positive-height drop, and no scenario damage. Incomplete trials and zero-fill controls cannot pass the selection test. More than one material can pass, and a lower drop height changes the glass example's result. No food-contact, drinking-water safety, hygiene, life-cycle sustainability or universal material recommendation is certified.

Five tests verify independent geometry/mass and gravity substitutions, water conservation, monotonic leakage, explicit completion criteria, height dependence, invalid ranges and all eight guide cases. Other gates remain pending.

Zero-height controls are explicitly labelled as no drop and cannot complete a comparison. Before impact no damage flag is assigned. An open support ring lets leakage reach the collector. Cutaways leave the full-cup calculation unchanged. Procedural studio reflections improve material visibility. The impact inspection camera works only after a completed positive-height drop and returns to the full overview. Manual water time is compressed 6×; manual drop time is replayed 6× slower. The 110-second guide holds final states for reading.
