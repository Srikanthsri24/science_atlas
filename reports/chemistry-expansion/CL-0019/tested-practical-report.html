Observe water drying from a shallow dish

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs · CL-0019 · Class 2

Aim

Compare evaporation from shallow dishes while changing one condition at a time.

Apparatus and principle

Shallow dish, water, timer and controlled temperature/humidity/air settings. Water vapour is invisible. The visual markers are schematic net-transfer cues, not visible steam or individual molecules. A maintained heat supply holds water and air at the same temperature.

Prediction

With the same starting water and conditions, more exposed area should transfer more water to the surrounding air. Higher humidity should reduce net evaporation.

Procedure

  1. Start with 10 mL water at 35 °C, humidity 25% and air setting 0.5.
  2. Record initial and two-hour model states for wet areas 20, 80 and 200 cm².
  3. Hold area constant and vary humidity, temperature or air movement separately.
  4. Try 100% humidity at equal water/air temperature, and an initially empty dish.
  5. Check remaining plus transferred mass equals starting mass. Write a conclusion and a model limitation.

Result and conclusion

At equal conditions, greater wet area increases net water transfer. At 80 cm², 4.875936 g remains and 5.124064 g has entered ambient air, totaling the starting 10 g. Saturated air at equal temperature gives zero net evaporation. This model assumes constant wet area, maintained temperature and humidity, and a chosen transfer coefficient; it is not a calibrated real drying time.

Precautions and limitations

Maintain one changed variable. Constant wet area is assumed until the ideal dry state; thin films and contact-angle changes are excluded. Temperature and surrounding humidity are externally maintained, so neither evaporative cooling nor local air saturation is solved. The chosen transfer coefficient includes background transfer at air setting zero; the setting is not a measured wind speed. Density 1 g/mL and latent heat 2.45 MJ/kg are reference approximations. Water transferred to the ambient reservoir is accounted mass, not collected condensate. Saturated conditions mean zero net transfer, not zero molecular exchange. This is not weather prediction or a calibrated physical drying time. Internal checks do not imply independent expert or official board certification.
