Fibre soaking, drainage and mass gain — practical record

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · CL-0112

Aim and prediction

Compare a matched soak/drain protocol, finite water and dry mass normalization.

Apparatus and principle

Matched dry coupons, separate finite water baths, lifting supports, drainage catch trays and a dry weighing pan with a 1 mg digital reference. Follow operational retained/drainable water accounts and subtract the same tare from dry and wet gross indications.

Procedure and recording instructions

  1. Write the aim and prediction. Use equal dry mass and record the coupon identity, selected construction, wetting and retention references.
  2. Record the dry weighing-pan tare and dry gross indication. Subtract tare to obtain indicated dry mass. Do not confuse grams with milligrams.
  3. Immerse each coupon in its own finite water bath for the same selected soak duration. Record bath volume; a small supply may limit uptake.
  4. Lift clear, transfer above the catch tray and allow the same selected drain duration. Keep collected drainage separate from remaining bath water.
  5. Transfer the drained coupon to a dry pan. Record wet gross indication and use the same tare. Keep water away from the balance mechanism.
  6. Calculate indicated wet minus dry mass, normalize by indicated dry mass and report the selected protocol. Compare this instrument result with the exact model prediction.
  7. Record observations for all four coupons at zero soak, increasing soak times and matched drain times. Change only one factor for each comparison.
  8. Write a conclusion using the recorded evidence. Distinguish dry-basis gain from wet-basis water fraction; mass gain alone does not identify internal absorption, inter-fibre retention or wearer comfort.

Result and conclusion

These selected operational references partition water between coupon, catch and bath. A 1 mg indication is distinct from model uptake, and does not isolate molecular fibre absorption.

Precautions and interpretation limits

  • All strong/drainable capacities and time constants are explicitly selected teaching references, not measured constants for cotton, wool, nylon or polyester.
  • Two operational water pools share one selected wetting fraction. They are not an experimentally resolved molecular-absorption and capillary-retention decomposition.
  • Only drainable water leaves during the selected 0–180 s drainage window. Strong-pool desorption, evaporation, humidity exchange, chemical changes and apparatus hold-up are omitted.
  • A construction factor changes the drainable capacity; finishes and textile structure can alter real results. No universal natural-versus-synthetic ranking is established.
  • Water density is selected as 1 g/mL. Coupon shape is a visual reference; the model does not predict fibre swelling or true microscopic pore geometry.
  • The digital reference has 1 mg divisions. Its 1 pg numerical half-division allowance is a computational boundary rule, not physical measurement uncertainty.
  • The 120-second guide stages a protocol. Selected soak and drain durations remain separate from presentation time. These immersed-liquid results are not humidity-based moisture regain.
