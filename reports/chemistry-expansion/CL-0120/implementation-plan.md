# CL-0120 — Solid ionic sample versus aqueous solution

Next sequential lab; not implemented or reviewed yet.

## Scientific design
Compare potassium chloride in a solid lattice and dissolved in water. Distinguish existence of charged ions from their translational mobility. Fixed lattice sites do not support bulk ionic charge transport in the school-level ideal solid model; dissolved ions can drift in opposite directions. Do not claim all real solids have exactly zero conductivity: defects, temperature and humidity are excluded. Do not depict dissolution as creating ions from neutral potassium/chlorine atoms. Hydration and lattice separation are explanatory representations, not molecular dynamics.

Use a low-voltage AC comparison with matching apparatus. Keep applied voltage independent of material conduction. Explicitly separate a qualitative solid idealization from a calibrated aqueous bulk reference; avoid inventing a numerical solid resistance or a dissolution-rate law. Reuse only established verified numerical reference functions where their assumptions actually apply. Include water-background comparison and disconnected/zero-excitation states. Apparatus, visible charge motion, normalized waveform and labels must agree.
