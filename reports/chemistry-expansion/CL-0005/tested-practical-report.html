Sort rough and smooth surfaces

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs · CL-0005

Aim

Compare three specified synthetic surfaces using equal-length scans and evidence-based relative grouping.

Apparatus and principle

Magnified surface model, scanning probe and height-profile display. Small height variations become visible under magnification. A profile samples one line, not the entire surface.

Prediction

The selected abrasive model may show more height variation than the selected glass model. I will compare equal scan conditions.

Procedure

  1. Inspect the magnified surface.
  2. Use a 10 mm line, 101 samples and equal scan direction and position for comparison.
  3. Run a complete scan before recording.
  4. Compare profiles, assign a relative group and state supporting evidence.
  5. Change direction separately to investigate the chosen wood grain.

Result and conclusion

Under equal central x-scan conditions, the selected glass model had the least height variation and the abrasive model the most. The chosen wood profile varied with direction. A different line sampled a different abrasive profile. These synthetic scans do not measure friction or how a real surface feels.

Precautions and limits

Compare equal lengths, spacing, direction and line position. Height statistics describe only the selected synthetic profile. Grouping is relative; no universal rough/smooth cutoff is imposed. The topographies are original teaching examples, not measured material data, tactile sensations, friction predictions or standards-compliant metrology. Vertical heights in 3D are exaggerated 50× relative to horizontal distances. The 2D plot uses a shared height-deviation scale. A report export does not certify the student's conclusion.
