# CL-0114 — dissolved and suspended material in water

Planned, not implemented or reviewed. Class 7 placement is editorial, pending official board mapping. Original catalogue wording retained: “Compare dissolved and suspended impurities in water”. Student wording should distinguish known added materials from unmeasured real-water contamination.

Investigation: four matched prepared references—water, dissolved sodium chloride, suspended insoluble reference particles, and their mixture. Compare ordinary size-selective filtration and a separately identified evaporation-to-dryness demonstration. Clear filtrate can retain dissolved salt. Neither appearance nor this virtual separation certifies drinking safety.

Apparatus target: supported feed vessel, finite poured aliquot, paper-lined funnel and receiver, separate dry-filter and evaporating-dish balance measurements. A magnified membrane inset is schematic and must not claim accurate ion/pore scale. Realistic tilt/transfer, flow into filter, retained particles and filtrate collection; evaporation plume is a staged water transfer to an explicitly tracked vapour account, not a predicted heating rate. Target guide 120–150 seconds, with enough time to explain each pool.

Model design to validate before coding: known soluble, nonvolatile salt; known inert insoluble solid; no oversaturation, adsorption, chemical reaction or dissociation particle-count claims. Finite filtration fraction and selected retention fraction; retained wet cake must explicitly retain its share of carrier and dissolved salt rather than disappearing water or claiming perfectly salt-free solids. Demonstrate separation limits with a bypass/particle-passage reference. Account for feed remaining, wet cake, collected filtrate and vapour plus dry residue. Dissolved concentration uses explicitly stated solution volume, not inferred from optical clarity. No calibrated NTU or universal TDS-to-conductivity conversion. Dry-filter mass gain includes only dry retained material after stated drying; separate dissolved salt in retained liquid when residue dries. Workings should include mg/L conversions, filtered volume, filter tare subtraction, receiver/residue tare and complete mass balances, with undefined percentage denominators handled honestly.

## Initial core implementation
`waterImpurities.ts` now implements four prepared controls and a150secondstageguide. Solution density is selected1g/mL, with suspended dry mass additional. The model uses finite nominal processed volume, selected carrier hold-up fraction, selected particle retention, optional pure-water rinse scaled to processedfraction, and an ideal well-mixed constant-carrier pore rinse. expm1 preserves tiny positive transferredsolute. No specific filter cutoff, empirical flow law or NTU calibration is claimed.
During drying, nonvolatile solute totals span dissolved/solid phases without a solubility allocation; do not label all salt dissolved at late drying stages. The model is not a solubility or heating-rate simulation. Residue includes any salt carried with retained liquid and any particles that pass the filter. Operational apparent mg/L is normalized to original processed sample volume, not diluted receiver volume. Dry weighing eligibility requires nonzero processed sample and complete drying. No apparatus hold-up beyond explicit cake carrier, adsorption, volatile solute or chemical change.
