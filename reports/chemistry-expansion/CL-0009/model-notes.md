# CL-0009 · Liquid shape and conserved volume

Internal engineering model; not an independent expert review or official board mapping.

A prescribed 20 mL/s metered transfer moves an initial 0–400 mL from a source into one of three ideal receivers. Every receiver is 10 cm tall. The narrow cylinder has internal radius 2 cm, capacity 40π ≈ 125.663706 mL. The rectangular vessel has 8 × 4 cm internal base, capacity 320 mL. The flared vessel has radius r(h) = 2 + 0.2h cm and capacity 280π/3 ≈ 293.215314 mL.

The receiver's retained water is min(transferred volume, capacity); any excess is caught separately. Source + receiver + caught overflow equals the initial volume at all supported times. Liquid height in the cylinder and prism follows their cross-sectional area. The flared height is obtained by monotone bisection of V(h) = π[4h + 0.4h² + (0.04/3)h³]. One mL equals one cm³.

The pump flow is imposed, not derived from source pressure or hydrostatic head. Liquid surfaces are level, water is incompressible, and pipe transit storage is neglected. Flow markers are schematic; no moving particles, splashes, free-jet trajectory, meniscus, wetting, evaporation or full fluid dynamics is claimed. The catch tray is broad and retains all overflow. Ideal internal dimensions are not calibrated apparatus measurements.

The 110-second guided comparison transfers the same 100 mL into all three shapes. Manual trials allow different initial volumes, pause/resume, time scrubbing and controlled overflow. Observation and exam-report calculations account separately for source, receiver, height and caught overflow. Equal volume can produce different liquid heights; height alone is not a volume measurement across different vessel shapes.
