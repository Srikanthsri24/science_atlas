Observe a liquid taking the shape of its container

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs · CL-0009

Aim

Compare the height and shape of the same volume of water in three vessels, and account separately for overflow.

Apparatus and principle

Metered reservoir, prescribed constant-flow pump, narrow cylinder, rectangular vessel, flared vessel, ruler, timer and catch tray. All receivers are 10 cm tall. Water volume is conserved; height depends on the vessel cross-section.

Prediction

The same 100 mL should rise highest in the narrow cylinder; changing container shape changes the height, not the amount of water.

Procedure

  1. Select an empty receiver and set 100 mL in the source.
  2. Start transfer; watch the source fall and receiver fill.
  3. Pause to record an intermediate state, then finish the transfer.
  4. Repeat with the same 100 mL for all three shapes.
  5. Try 200 mL in the narrow cylinder; record retained water and caught overflow separately.
  6. Include a zero-volume control and verify conservation for every trial.

Result and conclusion

Equal 100 mL volumes give different heights: about 7.958 cm narrow, 3.125 cm rectangular and 5.018 cm flared. When a receiver fills, excess water is caught separately. Source + retained water + caught overflow equals the initial volume, including the zero and 400 mL controls. This is prescribed metered flow; meniscus, splash and tube storage are neglected.

Precautions and model limits

Keep initial volume equal for a fair shape comparison. Read height from the receiver base and record overflow separately. A higher water level does not necessarily mean more water. The inlet is prescribed at 20 mL/s; this is a volume-conserving metered-transfer model, not a gravity-drain or general fluid simulation. Water is treated as incompressible with a level surface. Meniscus, wetting, waves, splashes, jet trajectories, evaporation and transit storage in the tube are neglected. Flow markers are schematic. Caught overflow is retained in a broad tray. Dimensions are ideal internal dimensions, not calibrated apparatus measurements.
