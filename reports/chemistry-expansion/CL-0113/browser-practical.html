Starch detection with iodine — virtual practical record

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · CL-0113

Aim and prediction

Compare known starch and glucose preparations with a no-reagent control. Change only delivered volume in the second trial.

Apparatus and principle

Four matched prepared samples, four finite iodine/potassium-iodide stocks, metering pipettes and mixing equipment. A qualitative association cue contrasts starch-containing references with controls. The numerical one-capacity-unit association reference is selected and not a calibrated starch assay.

Procedure and recording instructions

  1. State the aim and predict the contrast between prepared starch, half-starch plus glucose, matched glucose and water references. These are known preparations, not measurements of real food composition.
  2. Record sample volume and full-reference dry-solute concentration. Record iodine-equivalent reagent strength; the selected stock also contains ten times that molar iodide inventory.
  3. Withdraw a finite aliquot from each separate 2 mL stock. Record stock remaining and volume held in the pipette before delivery.
  4. Position the pipette, dispense the selected volume and mix. Keep initial no-iodine controls distinct from completed reagent tests; an undosed sample is not a negative starch result.
  5. Pause only after the guided mixing stage is complete before recording a result. In manual mode the numeric reference assumes complete mixing. Write your qualitative colour observation separately from the calculated reference cue.
  6. Calculate dry solute masses, transferred reagent inventories and nominal dilution using the recorded values. The extended association and inventory calculations explain a selected mathematical reference, not a calibrated analytical assay.
  7. Compare the controls and write a result and conclusion. State that reference colour does not establish an unknown food concentration, a visual detection limit or absence of all carbohydrates.
