Classify a material as element, compound or mixture

CL-0073 · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · Virtual practical

Aim

Classify defined gas reference samples using substance identities, bonded atom types and fixed composition. Distinguish molecular elements from compounds and mixtures.

Principle and apparatus

Virtual dilute-gas observation cube, magnified rigid ball-and-stick molecules, element key, particle counter and classification record. Defined identities supply particle evidence; a real jar appearance cannot establish chemical purity. Count nonzero distinct substances first. More than one is a mixture. For one substance, one atomic-number identity gives an elemental substance; two or more bonded element identities in fixed ratios give a compound. Oxygen mixed with ozone is a mixture of elemental substances although all atoms are oxygen. Unreacted hydrogen and oxygen are a mixture, not water.

Procedure

  1. Select the reference sample. State your prediction before recording the classification.
  2. Observe the moving entities. Inspect a molecule in 2D and 3D. Record the formula, bonded atom types and chemical identity; projected overlap does not show bonding.
  3. Count entities of each substance and atoms of each element. Record distinct substance and element counts.
  4. For two-component references, change the second count while keeping total entities fixed. Compare an interior mixture with both pure endpoints.
  5. Repeat with oxygen, water vapour, oxygen/ozone and unreacted hydrogen/oxygen. Explain why colour count or total atom count alone is insufficient.
  6. Apply the appropriate number, atom and mass fraction calculations. Ideal pressure and molecular amount/mass are optional teacher extensions, not measured laboratory quantities.
  7. Write a result and conclusion based on particle evidence. State the model limits.

Prediction and plan

<evidence> Compare substance identities first. A molecule may contain one element; oxygen/ozone can still be a mixture.
