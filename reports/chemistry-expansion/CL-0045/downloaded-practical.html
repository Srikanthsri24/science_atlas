Compare beaker and measuring-cylinder readings

Science Atlas360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · CL-0045 · Class4 editorial investigation

Aim

Compare reporting increments, settled readings and errors for two equal known reference aliquots. Distinguish resolution from calibration accuracy.

Apparatus and model

Original virtual100mL vessels: wide beaker with10mL divisions and narrow cylinder with1mL divisions. Each receives a separate equal aliquot at1mL/s from a finite source. Model concave surfaces conserve volume; scales are defined by their bottom position. Chosen scale bias affects both vessels. These are teaching specifications, not manufacturer tolerance classes.

Prediction

Equal aliquots should reach different heights. Finer cylinder divisions should reduce rounding bounds, but a common scale bias will remain.

Procedure

  1. Select a known equal reference aliquot and reporting convention. Keep scale bias zero for the first comparison.
  2. Start both transfers. Watch equal volume produce different liquid heights. Record source and receiver volumes.
  3. Wait for flow to stop before reading. Use the lowest point of each model concave meniscus and its own scale.
  4. Record both readings and reporting increments. Calculate signed error and relative error against the known reference.
  5. Compare nearest-mark and tenth-of-division rounding at the same reference volume.
  6. Repeat with a common nonzero scale bias. Distinguish reduced rounding bound from corrected accuracy.
  7. Write a conclusion supported by observations. State that a rounding-only bound is not total measurement uncertainty.

Result and conclusion

The cylinder had a smaller reporting increment and rounding-only bound. The chosen common scale bias still affected its reading, so finer divisions alone did not establish accuracy. Equal contained volumes reached different heights. These results apply to the stated ideal reference model; total uncertainty and real glassware calibration were not measured.

Precautions and limitations

Read a stable concave meniscus from its bottom with appropriate eye alignment and lighting. In this model readings become available when flow stops; no settling-delay solver is included. Prescribed curved surfaces,ideal cylindrical walls,lumped1mL/s transfer and deliberately chosen common scale bias are assumptions. No contact-angle dynamics,parallax,temperature expansion,jet inventory,drainage,evaporation or external calibration is solved. The reporting increment and half-increment bound describe rounding only. Smaller divisions do not establish total uncertainty,manufacturer accuracy or an independent certification. Repeating this deterministic model is not a statistical precision study.
