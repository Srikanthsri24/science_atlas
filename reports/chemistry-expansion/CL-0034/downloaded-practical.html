Follow evaporation and condensation in a closed system

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · Class 3 · CL-0034

Aim

Track100 g water through pool, invisible vapour and lid condensate, distinguishing stored water from cumulative evaporation, condensation and return.

Apparatus and principle

Sealed reference-headspace jar,100 g pool, temperature-held collecting lid and ideal internal return path. Initially the air is dry. The pool and bulk air share a maintained temperature; the lid has its own setpoint. Matter stays sealed while heat crosses the boundary. Chosen transfer coefficients and a first-order drain law are used; no fluid, thermal-transient or actual drop-detachment solver is claimed.

Prediction

A cooler lid will collect water once vapour pressure crosses its threshold. Faster return should reduce lid storage at matched time, without changing the total 100 g.

Procedure

  1. Prepare1.5 L initially dry headspace above100 g pool water. Set pool/air35°C, lid10°C and drain time constant60s.
  2. Record zero time. Run and pause before and after the predicted condensation threshold. Vapour is invisible; use its mass and pressure readouts.
  3. Record pool, vapour and lid water. Check their sum is100 g.
  4. Record cumulative evaporated, condensed and returned amounts separately. The same water can pass through a phase more than once.
  5. Repeat a fresh warm-lid control, then compare faster and slower return at the same time. Change one setting per trial.
  6. Apply the worked pressure, rate, reservoir and reference latent-heat substitutions. Write a result supported by observations and state a limitation.

Result and conclusion

The three inventories total 100 g in every trial. Warm and equal-temperature lids collected no water. At 300 s, faster return left 0.009270 g on the lid versus 0.049234 g with slower return. The chosen maintained-temperature and first-order transfer model is not a physical droplet-detachment or heat-flow experiment.

Precautions and limitations

Distinguish water inventories from cumulative transfers. A sealed jar is closed to matter, not automatically insulated. Temperatures, reference area/headspace, mixing, liquid density and reference latent heat2450J/g are approximations or maintained model settings. Reference headspace stays fixed despite tiny pool/lid volume shifts. No fog, visible vapour particles, CFD, heat-transfer field, actual droplet nucleation/coalescence or transit-water inventory is solved. The16 hemispherical sites encode shared lid storage at reference density1g/cm³; they are not counted real droplets or a detachment model. Moving arrows annotate phase/return fluxes, not particle trajectories. Guide flow time is 7.5×; manual time is 1×.
