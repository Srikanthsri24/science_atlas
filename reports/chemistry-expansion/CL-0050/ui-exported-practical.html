Investigate how surface area changes evaporation

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · CL-0050 · Class 4 editorial investigation

Aim

Compare evaporation from equal initial water amounts with different exposed areas while the model holds other conditions fixed. Distinguish mass loss,depth loss and finite drying.

Apparatus and model

Two original constant-area circular receivers,matched ideal 37°C surface controllers,reference water density 1 g/mL and an open surrounding-air sink. Uniform mass flux 0.2 kg/(m² h) is a chosen teaching parameter,not an empirical weather prediction. Representative latent heat 2428000 J/kg supports a latent-energy extension. Water vapour is invisible;any displayed tracers are illustrative.

Prediction

With equal initial water and matched prescribed flux,I expect the wider wet surface to lose more mass per physical hour. Both wet depths should fall equally under this model.

Procedure

  1. Choose equal starting water volume and two exposed areas. Record the different initial depths.
  2. Keep temperature,humidity,airflow and prescribed flux matched by assumption. Change only the area for the main comparison.
  3. Run the time-lapse and pause at selected physical hours. The 120 s guide compresses 24 physical hours into 90 moving seconds;presentation seconds are not physical hours. Manual 120-second playback spans 72 physical hours.
  4. Record remaining mass,evaporated mass,current depth and actual rate for both samples in each matched row.
  5. Compare equal areas and swapped areas. Under uniform flux,the two wet depths fall at the same rate even though different areas lose different masses.
  6. Continue to a dry-state case. Note that evaporation and latent power stop when a sample is empty;the initial area ratio does not describe cumulative losses indefinitely.
  7. Apply the substitutions below,check remaining plus escaped water,and write a supported conclusion.

Precautions and limitations

Use the stated physical hours,not time-lapse seconds. The same uniform flux and maintained 37°C are model assumptions,not measured room conditions. Constant density and area,an infinite air sink and ideal latent-heat replenishment are assumed. Boundary-layer shape,size-dependent airflow,humidity changes,surface cooling,sensible heat losses,receiver heat capacity,contact-line recession and condensation are omitted. Latent energy is not total electrical heater consumption. Evaporation is not boiling;water molecules remain water in air. The model does not predict a real tray's drying time or claim calibration/independent certification.
