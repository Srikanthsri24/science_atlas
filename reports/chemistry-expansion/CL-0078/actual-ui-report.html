CL-0078 · Prepare a solution of stated mass concentration

Aim,apparatus and method

Reference balance,weighing boat,water reservoir and idealised volumetric flask.

  1. Aim: prepare a solution with a stated mass concentration,using final solution volume in litres.
  2. Calculate required mass. Zero the reference balance and record weighed mass before transfer.
  3. Add a smaller initial amount of water. Transfer solid and rinse the weighing boat into the flask; record any remaining material.
  4. Wait until all transferred solid dissolves. In a physical laboratory,allow a warmed solution to return to the specified flask temperature before setting volume.
  5. Add water gradually to the final solution mark. Read the bottom of the real meniscus at eye level. This idealised model uses a flat calibrated reference surface. Adding the nominal flask volume as water is a deliberate error comparison.
  6. Stopper and invert several times in a physical laboratory. Here the mixing stage is a scheduled reference operation,not a fluid-dynamics or molecular-mixing prediction.
  7. Pause at settled stages and record time,mass,residue,final solution volume and concentration. Compare a correct preparation with missed rinsing,wrong mass or wrong volume.
  8. Substitute the observed dissolved mass and final solution volume into C = m/V. Report g/L,procedure validity,result and limitations.

Prediction and plan

Weigh the calculated mass,rinse quantitatively,dissolve and make the final solution to the mark. Compare missing rinse and premature filling.

Result,conclusion and precautions

At 2 g dissolved in 0.100 L,the concentration is 20 g/L. Water volume alone is not final solution volume. A missed rinse lowers the dissolved mass. Fixed-temperature reference;uncertainty is not modelled.

Generic undersaturated soluble reference,not a named chemical calibration. β=0.6 mL/g,solid density2 g/mL,water density1 g/mL and shrinking-radius cohort completion interval are chosen assumptions. Solution and dry-solid volumes are different. Fixed temperature,no heat of dissolution,evaporation,balance uncertainty or equilibrium prediction. A target concentration can coincide with incomplete quantitative transfer;these checks are reported separately. Mixing is a procedural completion flag.
