Aim

To compare spatial warming and heat conduction in equal copper,aluminium and stainless steel 304 rods,while reconciling supplied,removed and stored energy.

Student hypothesis and plan

Compare equal rods at common physical times. Change one condition at a time and use equal baths as a control. <safe plan>

Apparatus and preparation

Three equal cylindrical rods with ideal thermostatted end contacts,insulated lateral surfaces and quarter,midpoint and three-quarter reference thermometers. Each rod starts at 25°C;the cold thermostat remains at 25°C. Both thermostats are externally maintained reservoirs,not isolated finite bodies.

Procedure and observation instructions

  1. Aim: compare spatial warming and heat conduction through equal-sized copper,aluminium and stainless steel 304 rods under the same end temperatures.
  2. Record common rod length,diameter and hot-thermostat setting. All rods and the cold thermostat initially have 25°C. At physical time zero,the hot boundary is imposed. Lateral surfaces are insulated in this reference.
  3. Read the same quarter,midpoint and three-quarter positions on every rod. Record all three materials at equal physical intervals such as 0,30,60 and 90s. The 120s presentation clock is different from the physical clock.
  4. Retain both end powers. During warming,hot-end power exceeds cold-end power because energy accumulates in the rod. The thermostat heat meters integrate power over time;do not multiply the final power by elapsed time to estimate a transient total.
  5. Calculate cross-sectional area,mass,heat capacity,thermal resistance and the steady Fourier-law power. Compare measured model temperatures with the common long-time linear reference.
  6. Calculate stored energy from the cell-mean temperature. Verify supplied heat minus removed heat equals the increase stored in the rod. Retain the numerical residual rather than replacing it with an invented exact zero.
  7. Repeat while changing only diameter or length. In this insulated 1D model,diameter changes power and mass together without changing the temperature history;length changes both resistance and diffusion time.
  8. Set the hot thermostat to 25°C for a control. With equal temperatures,no rod warms and every net heat flow must be zero.
  9. Write a result that distinguishes conductivity from diffusivity. Temperature at one point during a transient does not by itself measure conductivity. Explain constant-property,ideal-contact and spatial-resolution limitations.
