Compare equal-volume samples by density

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · CL-0048 · Class 4 editorial investigation

Aim

Compare net masses of equal liquid volumes. Subtract each container's own tare and infer density from net mass divided by delivered volume.

Apparatus and model

Two original ideal digital balances, containers of identical internal radius 2 cm, separate finite reference-liquid dispensers and volume scales. Each controlled dispenser delivers 1 mL/s. Reference densities and empty-container masses are chosen model values; neither balance response nor commercial liquid properties are calibrated.

Prediction

For the same delivered volume,I expect the higher reference density to have greater net liquid mass. Different container tares may mislead gross comparisons.

Procedure

  1. Choose a common target volume and two reference densities. Note the empty mass of each container separately.
  2. Predict which liquid sample will have greater net mass. Do not use container appearance to infer density.
  3. Start paired transfer. Pause at selected physical times and check that both delivered volumes and liquid depths match.
  4. Read each ideal gross balance value. Subtract its matching container tare to obtain net sample mass.
  5. For a nonempty sample, divide net mass by the delivered volume, not the target volume before transfer is complete.
  6. Record matched trials with equal or different densities, and repeat using reversed empty-container masses. Compare net ordering with gross ordering.
  7. Apply the formulas below, check each liquid's separate source/receiver mass balance, and write a supported conclusion.

Result and conclusion

Matched volumes had equal liquid depths. Higher reference density gave greater net sample mass. A heavier A container reversed gross ordering without reversing liquid density. Empty samples had undefined inferred density. Ideal balances omit calibration uncertainty.

Precautions and limitations

Use the delivered volume and each container's own tare. Do not divide by zero for an empty sample. Equal heights establish equal volumes only because internal areas match here. Colours identify A and B and do not encode density. The model assumes incompressible liquids, ideal balances and quasi-static transfer; splashing, evaporation, thermal expansion, jet inventory, balance lag, physical measurement noise and calibration uncertainty are omitted. The supplies are separate and are not mixed. These model observations are not calibrated physical measurements or independent certification.
