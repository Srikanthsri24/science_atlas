Compare flexible and rigid objects

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs · CL-0006

Aim

Compare incremental bending of three specified strips at equal geometry and added load.

Apparatus and principle

Ideal clamp, identical 100 × 20 × 4 mm strips, added tip load and displacement readout. Under these conditions, greater bending indicates lower stiffness. No specimen is perfectly rigid.

Prediction

For equal strip geometry and added mass, the chosen flexible-polymer model should bend most. The ideal model should recover when unloaded.

Procedure

  1. Record the unloaded reference.
  2. Apply the same added mass to each strip and wait for the load change to finish or pause it deliberately.
  3. Record added mass, force and tip deflection.
  4. Investigate the largest supported load separately.
  5. Remove the load and compare recovery with the reference.

Result and conclusion

At equal geometry and 5 g added mass, the selected polymer model bent most, then wood, then steel. The 8 g polymer case bent more. Removing the load restored the unloaded reference in this ideal elastic model. The moduli are illustrative; creep, yielding and clamp compliance are not modelled.

Precautions and model limits

Keep geometry and added load equal. Read actual numerical deflection, not the magnified picture. Display bending is exaggerated 5×. Elastic moduli are chosen examples, not measured specimen properties. The small-deflection Euler–Bernoulli model reports incremental bending relative to the unloaded reference. Clamp compliance, self-weight baseline, creep, yielding, shear, torsion and dynamic oscillation are excluded. Load application is staged quasi-statically; no impact or falling-mass motion is solved. No engineering or material certification is implied.
