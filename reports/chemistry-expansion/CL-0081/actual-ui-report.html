CL-0081 · Compare solute recovery after evaporation

Aim,apparatus and method

Two tared dishes,reference drying chamber,cooling station,balance and solution catcher. Original generic virtual material;no named chemical calibration.

  1. Aim: compare dry solute recovery with and without a controlled loss of well-mixed solution.
  2. Start with equal known water and nonvolatile solute masses in two tared dishes. Keep the prescribed water-only evaporation rate equal.
  3. Dish A retains its contents. Dish B loses solution to a catcher at the selected rate while wet. This is a chosen handling-loss scenario,not a prediction that heating always causes splashing.
  4. Inspect clear solution,crystal formation,vapour water and the catcher. Record reference time and both water and solute inventories. Vapour contains no solute in this model.
  5. Compare a wet sample separately:cool it and subtract the tared dish reading. Label this apparent recovery;it includes retained water. Do not call a large wet mass a high dry yield.
  6. Continue the reference drying until both dishes have zero water. Remove to the cooling/weighing station and cool to at most30°C before reading. Transfer is frozen while cooling in this simplification.
  7. Subtract the separately rounded tare reading from each gross reading. Calculate100 × dry net mass / initial solute mass. Compare this reported value with the reference inventory and account for balance rounding.
  8. Explain why solute found in the catcher lowers dish recovery. Real practice uses suitable drying,cooling and repeated weighing for the material;two identical rounded readings alone do not prove absolute dryness.
  9. Write an exam-style report with aim,apparatus,method,observation table,formula with substitution,result,conclusion and limitations.

Prediction and plan

Compare equal preparations at one evaporation rate. Record the cooled wet comparison separately,then cool and weigh both dry residues. Account for lost solute in the catcher.
