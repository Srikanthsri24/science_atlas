Phase change and chemical identity

CL-0105 · Class 7 · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited

Aim and prediction

Compare H₂O phase amounts with retained MgO. Prediction: returning water phase energy preserves chemical products.

Apparatus and principle

Separate virtual ice/water and magnesium/oxygen reference stations. Ice and liquid remain H₂O; the selected reaction 2Mg+O₂→2MgO forms a different ionic substance when reagents are available. Returning water phase energy preserves reaction history. Chemical reference states are reported after assumed oxidation and cooling; ignition and chemical heat are not modeled.

Procedure and observation instructions

  1. Write an aim comparing chemical identity in a phase-change station and a separate reaction-account station. Predict what returning the phase energy will change, and what it will leave unchanged.
  2. Record the bath water, initial ice, initial magnesium and initial oxygen. The water station is a 0 °C phase-only system. The reaction station reports a prescribed oxidation reference state after cooling to 298.15 K; ignition, reaction heat and kinetics are outside this model.
  3. Start at zero phase energy and zero delivered magnesium. Add net phase energy and record the ice/liquid amounts. Use Q=mL and the selected 334 J/g fusion heat; temperature stays 0 °C within this phase-only range.
  4. Advance magnesium delivery and record stock metal, unreacted delivered metal, oxygen consumed and MgO formed. Apply 2Mg+O₂→2MgO with the finite limiting-reagent extent. The feed reference does not predict spontaneous room-temperature oxidation.
  5. Record the floating ice displacement and constant waterline envelope. Net liquid volume excludes the submerged ice. MgO amounts refer to ionic-solid formula units; H₂O keeps the same molecular identity in ice and liquid.
  6. Remove the phase energy without resetting the chemical preparation. Record a before/after pair. The water phase amounts return, but the reference reaction products are retained. Preparing fresh reagents is a new trial, not chemical reversal.
  7. Repeat with oxygen absent and with oxygen limiting. Do not claim a chemical change merely because material entered the reaction apparatus: the calculated new-substance flag is false when no MgO forms.
  8. Complete the energy, geometry, reactant, product and conservation tables. Show formula substitution with units, then write a result and student conclusion. Use chemical identity as the classification criterion; reversibility alone is not its definition.
