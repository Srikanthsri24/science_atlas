Compare dry sand and wet sand

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs · CL-0010

Aim

Compare release of dry and damp model sand slices at equal mass, angle and surface friction.

Apparatus and principle

Tilt tray, clamp, 20 g failure-plane slice, timer and 150 mm end stop. Chosen liquid bridges add an initial cohesion limit. The grain-contact inset is enlarged and schematic.

Prediction

At equal 40° angle, the damp model should resist sliding because of its chosen contact bridges; dry sand should slide.

Procedure

  1. Clamp the dry sample and set 40°.
  2. Release, pause to observe motion, then record its final position.
  3. Repeat with damp sand at the same 40°.
  4. Test damp sand at 55° and a horizontal control.
  5. Compare evidence and calculate the holding limits.

Result and conclusion

At equal 40° angle and 20 g mass, dry sand slides while the chosen damp slice resists because its initial bridge cohesion adds to the friction limit. Damp sand fails at 55° and 60°. Horizontal and threshold controls behave as predicted. The enlarged grains represent a plug model with chosen bridge count, not a calibrated real-sand collapse angle.

Precautions and limitations

Compare equal mass, angle and friction. Distinguish clamped and released states. Motion is replayed 6× slower; recorded time is model time. This is an illustrative failure-plane and sliding-plug model, not a discrete-grain simulation or calibrated soil test. The damp slice has 500 chosen load-bearing contact bridges; this is not a measured water-content percentage. Bridges rupture at failure; reformation, grain rearrangement, drainage, vibration and detailed liquid-bridge geometry are excluded. Static and sliding friction use the same chosen coefficient. Grains and bridge insets are enlarged; force arrows are schematic. Do not infer a universal wet-sand collapse angle.
