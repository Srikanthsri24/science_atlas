# CL-0010 · Dry and damp sand — model under development

The interface, 2D/3D visualization and practical record have passed internal functional review. Evidence is recorded in review.json; no independent expert or official-board certification is claimed.

A chosen 20 g failure-plane slice is compared on the same tilt tray with the same friction coefficient tan(30°). A dry slice has no capillary bridges. A damp slice has 500 chosen load-bearing bridges. The contact-bridge estimate is 2πrγ for complete wetting, with grain radius 0.25 mm and surface tension 0.072 N/m. These are illustrative parameters, not measured specimen properties or a prescription for water content.

Downhill weight is mg sin θ. Normal force is mg cos θ. Before release, the static resistance limit is μmg cos θ plus bridge cohesion. Failure occurs only when downhill weight exceeds this limit. The dry critical angle is 30°; the chosen damp condition withstands 40° but fails before 50°. At failure the load-bearing bridges rupture, and the plug moves with Coulomb friction until it reaches a perfectly inelastic end stop 150 mm away.

This is a simplified failure-plane and sliding-plug model. It is not a DEM simulation, a calibrated soil-strength test, a full liquid-bridge geometry solver or a water-content curve. Grain rearrangement, bridge reformation, pressure-dependent wetting, fluid drainage and vibration are excluded. A future visualization must distinguish a held sample during tilt from a released slice, and must identify enlarged grain/bridge insets as schematic.

Four model tests verify bridge/weight substitution, controlled dry/damp comparison, higher-angle failure and stop bounds, invalid inputs, and the held-tilt/release guide stages. Other review gates remain pending.

Motion is replayed six times slower in the guide and manual trial. Physical model time, angle and travel are recorded independently of presentation time. The guide holds the sample with a visible clamp while tilting, then releases it. Paused and clamped observations are explicitly distinguished in the practical record.
