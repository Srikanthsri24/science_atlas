Separate rice and loose husk by winnowing

Science Atlas360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · CL-0039 · Class4 editorial investigation

Aim

Investigate how prescribed airflow changes where rice and loose husk references land. Compare near-mat purity with rice recovery.

Apparatus and model

Virtual feeding basket, uniform horizontal airflow, near/far collection mats and an outside-floor ledger. Quadratic vector drag plus gravity acts on reference kernels. Forty-eight magnified trajectory markers each represent 1000 independent identical kernels; they are not physical clumps. Initial reference masses:600g rice and48g husk. Effective areas, masses and drag coefficients are illustrative, not calibrated crop or winnower data. Ideal contact surfaces hold landed material; no bounce, resuspension, lift, turbulence, particle interaction or orientation dynamics is solved.

Prediction

Loose husk should drift farther in the prescribed air. Still air will not separate the mixture, and stronger wind may also carry rice away from the near mat.

Procedure

  1. Set wind, release height and near-mat divider. Record the initial source masses.
  2. Start the slow feed. At equal model times, pause and account for source, airborne, near, far and outside material.
  3. Wait for the final kernels to land before reporting final recovery. Label earlier results provisional.
  4. Repeat in still air, at the reference wind and at a stronger wind. Change one variable at a time.
  5. Compute near-mat purity=100×near rice/(near rice+near husk), and overall rice recovery=100×near rice/initial rice. An empty mat has undefined purity.
  6. Explain the role of both mass and air resistance. Support a conclusion with observations and state a limitation.

Result and conclusion

Winnowing depends on both mass and air resistance. Compare purity and rice recovery at equal height, wind and divider settings. Count source, airborne, near, far and outside fractions. These are reference-model results; fixed effective drag parameters and ideal capture do not measure farm performance.

Limitations and interpretation

Winnowing separates already loose grain and chaff; this model does not remove attached husks. Effective projected area and drag coefficient are fixed references rather than a tumbling solver. Wind is prescribed everywhere and is not predicted from fan RPM. Buoyancy, collisions, shielding, turbulence, bounce and resuspension are neglected. Reference cohorts estimate bulk mass under independent-kernel assumptions. Numerical digits and perfect reference separation are not measured farm performance. Material outside the collection zone remains in the mass budget. Stronger wind can carry useful rice into the wrong fraction.
