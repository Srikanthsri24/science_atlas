Aim

Compare water passing through selected sheets under controlled conditions.

Apparatus

Upper reservoir sealed to a sheet over a 5 cm² opening, clamp, lower collecting cup, water and timer.

Principle

A sheet can act as a barrier to water passing through it. Compare equal conditions and distinguish an intact sheet from a punctured one. No leak in a finite trial is evidence for those conditions, not a universal material guarantee.

Prediction

The intact plastic and foil sheets should keep the lower cup dry. A puncture may let water through.

Procedure

  1. Select an intact sheet and equal starting water volume.
  2. Start a timed trial. Observe the upper level and lower collecting cup.
  3. Record the same elapsed time for every sheet.
  4. Compare an intact and a punctured barrier separately.

Teacher extension: formulas and substitution

After the chosen wetting delay, Q = Gh and h = V/A. Integrating dV/dt = −GV/A gives V = V₀ exp(−Gt/A). 1 mL = 1 cm³.

Trial 1: Opening area A = 5 cm². Effective time = max(0, 60.0 − 8) = 52.0 s. Remaining V = V₀e^(−Gt/A) = 20.0 × e^(−0.025 × 52.0 / 5) = 15.421 mL. Collected = 20.0 − 15.421 = 4.579 mL. Head = V/A = 15.421 / 5 = 3.084 cm. Conductance G is in mL s⁻¹ cm⁻¹. Calculations use unrounded values.

Result and conclusion

With 20 mL, a sealed 5 cm² opening and 60 seconds, the intact plastic and foil models collected no water. Cloth collected more than paper. A puncture changed the plastic result. This finite illustrative test does not certify every real sheet.

Precautions and limits

Keep area, starting volume and test duration equal; seal the edge and distinguish holes from intact sheets. No leak in a finite test is evidence for the chosen sample under those conditions. Parameters are illustrative, not measured material properties. Absorption, swelling, tearing, capillary effects and pressure-dependent deformation are not solved. Droplets are explanatory markers, not measured particle volumes.
