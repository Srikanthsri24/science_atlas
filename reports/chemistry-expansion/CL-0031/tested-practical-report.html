When does added salt stop dissolving?

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · Class 3 · CL-0031

Aim

Add measured salt portions and distinguish finite-time residue from equilibrium excess beyond a fixed-temperature reference capacity.

Apparatus and principle

Reference water cell, tared model balance, measured 5 g dose cup, 60 g salt supply and a common clock at 25 °C. Approximate capacity is 0.36 g per mL starting water. Salt enters over 2 model seconds at 2.5 g/s, followed by a 120-second hold. The chosen first-order dissolution coefficient is 0.05 s⁻¹. All earlier portions receive their full transfer and hold before the selected portion. This model uses exact linear-input and constant-target solutions; it is not calibrated physical timing.

Prediction

QA: in 100 mL water, 7 complete 5 g portions should remain below the 36 g approximate capacity; 8 portions should exceed it and leave at least 4 g at equilibrium.

Procedure

  1. Begin with 100 mL starting water and zero added salt. Record the initial state and predict when persistent excess will appear.
  2. Transfer a 5 g portion. Note transferred mass and the changing balance reading during transfer.
  3. Pause at matching hold times and record added, dissolved and solid masses. Complete the 120-second hold before the next dose.
  4. Repeat through eight portions. Compare 40 g added with the 36 g reference capacity. Separate equilibrium excess from transient residue.
  5. Repeat as a fresh experiment with 200 mL water. Keep dose size, temperature and model coefficients fixed.
  6. Apply the formulas to recorded inputs, check salt conservation, and write a supported conclusion with one limitation.
