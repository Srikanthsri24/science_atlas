Compare clay, paper and metal after wetting

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs · CL-0021 · Class 2

Aim

Distinguish water absorbed into selected intact coupons from surface water and compare wet mass after equal soak/drain times.

Apparatus and principle

Selected uncoated paper, porous unfired clay and nonporous metal coupons; separate water baths, drainage cup, holder, balance and timer. Each coupon has its own finite water reservoir. A wet surface does not prove bulk absorption.

Prediction

The selected porous paper and clay should retain absorbed water after drainage. Nonporous metal may be wet on its surface without absorbing water into its bulk.

Procedure

  1. Record the selected coupon's dry reference mass.
  2. Use 10 mL water and a 60 s soak for each separate trial.
  3. Lift and transfer the intact coupon in the holder; collect surface drainage for 30 s.
  4. Transfer to the balance and record wet mass, absorbed water and retained film.
  5. Repeat with the other selected coupons at equal timings.
  6. Compare bulk-only and apparent mass gain, close the water balance, and write a conclusion with a specimen/model limitation.

Result and conclusion

At equal 60 s soak and 30 s drainage, selected paper absorbs 3.926737 g and clay absorbs 2.593994 g; the selected nonporous metal absorbs zero. Surface film adds to wet mass and must not be mistaken for bulk uptake. For paper, bath + absorbed + surface + collected drainage equals the starting 10 g water. These chosen intact coupons do not predict erosion, strength failure or long-term corrosion.

Precautions and limitations

Compare equal soak/drain times. The balance display excludes the holder's mass (tare). Selected intact coupons have chosen capacities and rates, not universal material data. The short experiment excludes erosion, lost fibres, dissolved solids, rust, strength failure and evaporation. The tiny adhered film remaining after the standardized drain is retained during weighing. Water density 1 g/mL is a reference approximation. Apparatus is schematic; numeric mass/volume readings govern the balance, not pixel dimensions. Do not infer mechanical strength, long-term corrosion or every clay/paper/metal grade from uptake alone. Internal engineering checks do not imply independent expert or official board certification.
