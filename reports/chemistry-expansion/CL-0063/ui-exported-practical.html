Neutralizing an acidic solution

CL-0063 · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · Virtual practical

Aim

Follow metered sodium-hydroxide solution into known hydrochloric acid. Compare acid excess, stoichiometric equivalence and base overshoot using calculated ideal pH and an elapsed titration curve.

Principle

HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O. Net ionic shorthand: H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O. Sodium and chloride remain dissolved spectator ions. The solutions are clear; pH is calculated from an ideal equilibrium model, not inferred from physical colour.

Apparatus and procedure

Calibrated virtual burette, metering valve, sample vessel, timer and ideal pH readout. Record acid volume/concentration, base concentration, planned charge, aliquot count, interval and initial burette reading. Run, pause and record before, near and after the target volume. A falling aliquot has left the burette but has not entered the vessel: subtract it from the reading change. Repeat no-addition, water-dilution and no-acid controls. Use smaller effective aliquots to bracket equivalence; never snap an observation to pH7. Record result, conclusion and limitations.

Result, conclusion and limitations

Burette delivery & pH must be interpreted with the model assumptions.

Complete dissociation of dilute strong acid/base, additive drawing volumes, fixed25°C, ideal ion activities and retained water autoionization. Whole aliquots mix/react instantly after contact. No heat balance, finite mixing kinetics, CO₂ uptake, splashing, pH-meter calibration/response or uncertainty model. Valve/nozzle hold-up and any probe displacement are omitted drawing references. Equivalence is a stoichiometric target; indicator endpoint is a different observation. Class placement is editorial; quantitative formulas are teacher extensions. This virtual model is not a physical handling procedure for unsupervised students.
