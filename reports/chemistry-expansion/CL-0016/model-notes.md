# CL-0016 — Oil and water forming layers

The original model describes a metered oil transfer into an initially water-filled cylindrical jar. It assumes immediate immiscible layers: immiscibility keeps phases separate, while density determines vertical order. A density difference is not itself the cause of immiscibility.

Chosen reference densities are 1.00 g/mL water and 0.85 g/mL oil, not calibrations for every oil or temperature. Water is 50–250 mL, source oil 0–150 mL, metered flow 0–10 mL/s and model time 0–30 s. The jar's internal radius is 3 cm and height 15 cm (capacity approximately 424.115 mL). The maximum selected contents are 400 mL; overflow is outside this control range.

Delivered oil is min(source oil, flow × time). Reservoir oil plus jar oil equals the starting oil. Water height and volume stay fixed during each transfer. Each layer height is its volume divided by πr², using mL = cm³. Tared liquid mass in the jar plus remaining source oil mass is conserved. Empty vessels, pump and tubing masses are excluded. Teacher gauge pressure is ρ_w g h_w + ρ_o g h_o, with cm converted to m and densities in kg/m³. Atmospheric pressure is excluded.

A zero-oil control must not be described as two visible layers even when its empty transfer is mathematically complete. Zero flow leaves positive source oil in the reservoir. Both controls have explicit frontend explanations and no oil band in the jar.

The apparatus uses a metered pump, reservoir, delivery tube and receiving jar. Both views decrease the source oil level as the upper jar layer grows. The 3D vessel shapes share one scale; reservoir radius 2.5 cm and internal height 8 cm accommodate the 150 mL maximum. The jar and reservoir sit on the bench with supported bases. Moving stream and tube markers indicate transfer only: stream/tube transit volume, resolved hydraulics, menisci, surface tension, temperature change, evaporation, entrained air, dissolution and emulsion kinetics are excluded. Phase colours aid visibility. Real agitation can produce temporary droplets or emulsions.

The guide lasts 110 seconds and compares three oil amounts.
