Observe oil and water forming layers

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs · CL-0016 · Class 2

Aim

Observe separate layers during a controlled oil pour and account for each phase's volume and mass.

Apparatus and principle

Cylindrical jar of internal radius 3 cm and height 15 cm, water, separate oil reservoir, metered outlet and model timer. Immiscibility keeps the phases separate; density determines their order. Chosen reference densities: water 1.00 g/mL and oil 0.85 g/mL. These values are not a calibration for every oil. Mass readings are liquid-only (tared); empty vessels, pump and tubing are excluded.

Prediction

Oil should remain a separate phase above water. Transferring it should not change the water volume or the total liquid mass.

Procedure

  1. Set 100 mL water and 100 mL source oil. Record before pouring.
  2. Set 5 mL/s and record halfway through a 20 s pour, then after completion.
  3. Compare larger water and oil volumes. Keep track of oil left in the reservoir.
  4. Try a zero-flow control and a zero-oil control; distinguish no oil layer from a completed positive-volume pour.
  5. Record volumes, heights and masses. Explain phase separation and a limitation of immediate ideal layering.

Result and conclusion

In the 100 mL water / 100 mL oil trial, tared liquid mass in the jar increases from 100 g to 185 g while total liquid mass remains 185 g. Oil forms the upper phase at the chosen lower density. Immiscibility and density explain different things. Immediate separation excludes real temporary droplets and emulsions.

Precautions and limitations

Use a gentle pour and distinguish immiscibility from density. In reality, stirring may produce temporary droplets or emulsions; this model assumes immediate separated phases at constant reference density. It excludes dissolution, mixing, temperature change, evaporation, meniscus, surface tension, entrained air and resolved flow or stream transit volume. The maximum 400 mL contents fit the jar's approximately 424.115 mL capacity, so overflow is outside the selected range. Flow rate and reference oil properties are chosen parameters. Do not taste laboratory samples. Internal engineering checks do not imply independent expert certification or official board mapping.
