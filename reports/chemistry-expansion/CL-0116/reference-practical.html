Formal electron transfer and charge conservation — practical record

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · CL-0116

Aim and prediction

Predict and compare formal electron gain, loss and charge conservation.

Apparatus and principle

Virtual atomic reference, electron-counting reservoir with a fixed positive background, and a separate travelling-electron account. This is a counting investigation, not physical ion preparation or a measured reaction.

Procedure and recording instructions

  1. State the aim: compare formal electron gain and loss while keeping atomic number and mass number fixed. Predict electron counts and the sign of charge before running the guide.
  2. Record the known isotope and selected completed net electron gain. Positive gain means electrons added to the atomic account; a negative value means electrons lost. This sign is opposite to the resulting atomic charge.
  3. Start with neutral atomic and reference reservoir accounts. The reservoir has twenty electrons and a fixed positive background of twenty elementary-charge units; it is a bookkeeping reference, not an identified chemical species.
  4. Pause during transfer and record a snapshot. Count atomic, reservoir and travelling electrons separately. A travelling electron belongs to neither endpoint account until arrival. Keep the snapshot label and guide time in your notebook.
  5. Calculate each charge in elementary-charge units, then substitute e0 = 1.602176634 × 10^-19 C. Sum all three charges and all electron counts. A snapshot can conserve charge without being the selected completed endpoint.
  6. Continue to verification and record the final endpoint. Compare it with a zero-transfer control and an example with the opposite transfer direction. Proton and neutron counts should not change for the same isotope.
  7. Write observations, result and conclusion. Distinguish a travelling snapshot from a verified endpoint. Formal electron accounting does not establish ion stability, orbital configuration, reaction mechanism, ionization energy or physical electron speed.

Result and conclusion

All three electron and charge accounts are needed during transfer. Distinguish intermediate snapshots from verified endpoints; no stability or reaction inference.

Precautions and interpretation limits

  • Selected known H–Ar nuclear references; generic gain/loss ranges are arithmetic exploration. Formal charge labels do not claim every isolated ion exists, is stable or has a particular ground-state configuration.
  • The fixed reservoir positive background is charge bookkeeping, not a nucleus, proton collection, chemical material or specified electrode. Total reference charge is zero; no chemical reaction or energy source is modelled.
  • Counting grids, token sizes and transfer arcs are arbitrary display choices. No orbitals, electron positions, measured paths, transfer rates, ionization energies or affinities are computed.
  • Proton and neutron identities and integer mass number stay fixed. This does not claim atomic mass is unchanged when electrons are gained or lost. Mass number is not atomic mass in grams or u.
  • Recorded guided snapshots must match the selected target and time. An intermediate snapshot is not a verified endpoint, even if all charges sum to zero. Manual observations are completed reference endpoints only.
  • Integer counts are exact; charge uses the exact elementary-charge constant with floating-point representation. The 150-second guide stages prediction, transfer, verification and interpretation rather than physical kinetics.
