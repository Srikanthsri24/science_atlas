Investigate floating layers of immiscible liquids

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · CL-0047 · Class 4 editorial investigation

Aim

Observe a lighter immiscible oil layer above water, measure layer depths, and account for liquid transferred from a finite source. Teacher extension: investigate hydrostatic pressure at different probe heights.

Apparatus and assumptions

Virtual 250 mL cylinder of internal radius 2.5 cm, controlled-plunger oil dispenser delivering 1 mL/s, height scale and non-displacing virtual pressure probe. Water reference density is 1 g/mL; chosen oil reference density is 0.8–0.95 g/mL. Colours identify phases and are illustrative.

Prediction

I predict the chosen immiscible oil will form an upper layer while the amount of water remains fixed.

Procedure

  1. Choose the water volume, oil target and reference oil density. Record a prediction before pouring.
  2. Start the transfer. Pause at selected times and note delivered oil and oil remaining in the source.
  3. Measure water and oil depths from the cylinder floor and interface. Record water depth separately from total surface height.
  4. Keep the liquid amounts fixed and move the probe through water, the interface, oil, the free surface and air. Pause before recording each trial.
  5. Compare matched trials by changing one variable at a time. Record the chosen parameters, readings and a written observation.
  6. Substitute recorded values into the formulas below. Compare base force with contained liquid weight, then write a supported conclusion.

Precautions and limitations

Use recorded physical pour time, not presentation time. Convert cm to m and g/mL to kg/m³ before calculating pressure. At the free surface or in air, liquid gauge pressure is zero; atmospheric pressure is excluded. Density contrast determines stable layer order only after immiscibility is assumed. This model does not predict molecular mixing, emulsions, temperature-dependent commercial oil properties, curved interfaces, capillary pressure, splashing, jet inventory or wall films. The controlled dispenser is not an unregulated gravity drain. The probe is virtual and displaces no liquid. Flat, quasi-static layers and negligible air weight are assumed. These model observations are not physical measurements or independent certification.
