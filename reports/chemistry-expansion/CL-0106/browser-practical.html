Conditions required for iron corrosion

CL-0106 · Class 7 · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited

Aim and prediction

Compare matched iron specimens with water and oxygen excluded separately. Record the chosen reference assumptions.

Apparatus and principle

Three matched virtual iron specimens compare water and accessible oxygen availability. The selected reference account requires both environmental conditions. A chosen progress law and hydrate composition support a separate material calculation; they are not measured corrosion data.

Procedure and observation instructions

  1. Write an aim and predict the difference between wet oxygen-containing, ideal oxygen-excluded and dry controls. Match initial iron mass, nail dimensions and the comparison time.
  2. Record the initial water and accessible oxygen budget. The oxygen budget is an externally specified model account, not a measured dissolved concentration or a quantity inferred from tube headspace. The oxygen-excluded and dry preparations are ideal boundaries.
  3. Record the initial state before advancing model days. Distinguish metal remaining from the selected product reference. Keep k and hydration x visible in the preparation record; neither is inferred from the appearance of a real specimen.
  4. Advance the illustrative time with the same selected k for all controls. A zero-rate reference gives no product even when both environmental conditions are available. Do not interpret that chosen control as evidence that real corrosion has a universal zero rate.
  5. Record each matched control at the same time. Note water availability, accessible oxygen, converted iron, retained specimen mass and calculated product. Colour and highlighted areas are schematic evidence markers, not measured corrosion coverage or a corrosion-rate calibration.
  6. For an oxygen-limited comparison, prepare a smaller accessible oxygen budget and repeat. Compare the finite material limit separately from the chosen target progression.
  7. Show formula, substituted values and units. The selected overall account is 4Fe + 3O₂ + 2xH₂O → 2(Fe₂O₃·xH₂O). It represents a chosen hydrate equivalent rather than a unique composition for every real rust sample.
  8. Write the result and a justified conclusion using the matched controls. State that real observations, composition and rate require an actual experiment. Include limitations and distinguish computational residuals from measurement error.

Precautions and model limitations

  • The reference progression 1−exp(−kt) is chosen, not empirically calibrated. Seven simulated days in the guide do not establish how much real rust forms in seven days. No temperature, dissolved-oxygen transport, electrolyte, pH or passivation law is solved.
  • The variable hydrate composition is selected as Fe₂O₃·xH₂O. Real corrosion products vary. At x=0 this is a dry-oxide accounting limit, not a claim that ordinary rust contains no water. Water is still required by the environmental gate.
  • The finite accessible oxygen pool may include a connected supply. It is not all dissolved in the preparation water and is not a computed gas pressure, oxygen solubility, air composition or headspace measurement.
  • The analysed boundary contains iron, accessible oxygen and water. Unchanged inert gases, oil, drying agent and apparatus tare are excluded. No gas pressure, rust density, rust expansion or total physical tube mass is predicted.
  • All calculated product is retained with the specimen. Dissolved intermediates, flaking and losses are omitted. Apparent specimen mass gain includes selected hydration water as well as oxygen uptake.
  • Equivalent pure-iron geometry uses density 7.87 g/mL and a pointed nail with a 0.3 cm tip and 0.12 cm head thickness. It does not predict the microstructure or local pitting of a real steel nail. Prepared waterlines do not predict displacement by variable-volume rust.
  • Displayed digits are computational, not measurement precision. Conservation residuals near floating-point precision are diagnostics. This is a virtual reference record, not a certificate of a real laboratory result.
