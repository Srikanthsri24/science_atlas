Observe ice changing into liquid water

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs · CL-0011

Aim

Distinguish warming ice, melting at constant temperature and warming liquid water in an ideal insulated calorimeter.

Apparatus and principle

Prescribed heater, ideal distributed thermal contact, supported ice block, perforated shelf, meltwater collector, temperature display and energy graph. The shelf keeps ice above the collected liquid. Q = Pt supplies sensible and latent heat.

Prediction

Ice should first warm to 0 °C, then melt at constant temperature while energy continues to enter. Total water mass should stay equal to the initial ice mass.

Procedure

  1. Set 100 g ice at −5 °C and a 60 W heater; record the initial state.
  2. Start heating. Pause and record before melting.
  3. Record two times on the 0 °C plateau; compare melted mass and input energy.
  4. Continue to 600 s and record the fully melted state.
  5. Compare a zero-power control and a larger ice mass.
  6. Reconcile both total mass and supplied energy.

Result and conclusion

Heat first warms ice, then supplies latent energy while the ideal mixture stays at 0 °C, then warms the liquid. For 100 g at 60 W for 600 s: 1050 J warms ice, 33400 J melts it and 1550 J warms water. Total mass remains 100 g. Greater initial mass or lower heater power leaves some ice at 600 s. This model excludes heat losses and gradients.

Precautions and model limits

Keep initial mass and heater power controlled. Do not equate constant temperature with no energy transfer. This is an ideal insulated lumped-enthalpy model with chosen constant properties: c_ice = 2100 J/(kg K), L = 334000 J/kg and c_water = 4180 J/(kg K). Gradients, calorimeter heat capacity, heat loss, supercooling, evaporation and pressure effects are excluded. The ice starts at −5 °C. The supported range stays below 4 °C and does not model boiling. Constant reference densities determine displayed phase volumes; the supported block shrinks without floating. Visual drips are schematic and omit transit mass; rack displacement is neglected. Manual playback compresses time 15×; the guide stages its timing for explanation. Recorded time is model time.
