Investigate volume displacement by an insoluble solid

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · Class 3 · CL-0036

Aim

Find an insoluble solid’s volume from full immersion, distinguishing partial immersion and correcting for captured overflow.

Apparatus and principle

Reference-area graduated vessel, water, suspended dense solid, slow lifting stand and overflow receiver. An immersed solid excludes water. The rise in the vessel reading plus water collected outside the vessel equals currently immersed solid volume. Only full immersion measures the whole solid volume.

Prediction

The water reading should rise as the solid enters. A partial immersion gives only immersed volume. With full immersion, the rise plus any collected overflow should equal the whole solid volume. Overflow should stay collected after lifting.

Procedure

  1. Prepare 50 mL water and the selected 20 cm³ model specimen. Record the initial reading with the solid clear above the vessel.
  2. Lower slowly. Pause during partial immersion and record the current reading, whether the solid is fully submerged, and any collected overflow.
  3. At the immersed hold, read at eye level and record V1, V2 and captured overflow. Keep the solid clear of the vessel base and walls.
  4. Calculate immersed volume=V2−V1+captured overflow. Use this as whole solid volume only when fully submerged.
  5. Lift clear and record again. Water collected by overflow stays in the receiver; it does not return automatically.
  6. Use a fresh 80 mL/30 cm³ overflow trial and compare partial/full/withdrawn states. Compare specimen shapes at matched volume and water settings. State result, conclusion and a limitation.

Result and conclusion

For the fully immersed 20 cm³ reference sphere, 70 − 50 = 20 cm³. With a 30 cm³ specimen and 80 mL starting water, 100 − 80 + 10 = 30 cm³; ignoring overflow gives the wrong 20 cm³ result. Partial immersion measures only the underwater part. After lifting from the overflow trial, 70 mL stays in the vessel and 10 mL remains in the receiver, conserving 80 mL water. Equal-volume specimens gave the same corrected volume but different forces. The ideal level, chosen density, negligible suspension volume and quasistatic motion are model limitations.

Precautions and limitations

Use a dense, insoluble, non-absorbing specimen without trapped bubbles. Read the reference level at eye height. Avoid losing overflow; otherwise a simple reading difference underestimates volume. Do not substitute partial immersed volume for whole volume. The vessel has ideal 16 cm² cross-section and 100 mL capacity; water density is a reference 1 g/cm³. Specimen densities and smooth geometric shapes are chosen references. The suspension volume is neglected. Slow prescribed motion is quasistatic: no acceleration, drag, meniscus, wetting films, splash, drainage transit inventory or calibrated instrument uncertainty is solved. Overflow is assigned immediately to the receiver. Rendered mesh surfaces approximate analytic curved geometry; readings use the analytic volume, not pixel measurements. Guide time is 1.5×; manual time is 1×. Internal consistency review is not independent expert or official-board certification.
