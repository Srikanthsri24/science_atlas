Aim

Compare selected indicator reference cards and distinguish qualitative matching from independently specified pH and ideal calculations.

Student hypothesis and plan

Compare selected references under the same protocol.

Apparatus,controlled variables and procedure

  1. Aim: compare prepared cabbage-indicator teaching references with an investigation, while distinguishing independently specified pH from evidence available through colour matching.
  2. Apparatus: five separate known-pH reference tubes, one investigation tube, a finite metered indicator reservoir, a clean delivery tip, white comparison cards and a notebook. The selected teaching cards are original illustrations, not measured calibration standards.
  3. Record the equal initial sample volume, selected indicator dose and independently specified investigation pH. Keep each tube independent. The reference conditions are ideal maintained pH; indicator loading and real buffer chemistry are not calculated.
  4. Meter the same selected indicator amount into each reference separately. Record delivered fraction, actual volume and whether the protocol is complete. Do not read a partial-dose hatching pattern as a real dye colour.
  5. Prepare the investigation with the same selected dose and viewing conditions. Compare only after all six selected additions are complete.
  6. Record every reference category, the investigation category and all matching selected reference pH values. The shared pink cards at pH2 and pH4 deliberately demonstrate ambiguity. Matching listed cards does not prove that an unknown lies in the continuous interval between them.
  7. For the optional ideal calculations, convert microlitres to millilitres, add actual delivered volume and account for the finite remaining stock. Calculate H⁺ from the independently imposed pH, pOH=14−pH and OH⁻ from pOH at ideal25°C.
  8. Compare two independently specified pH values using Href/Htest=10^(pHtest−pHref). A two-unit difference means a100-fold ideal concentration ratio even when the chosen teaching shade is shared. Do not average pH arithmetically or infer pH from an arbitrary RGB value.
  9. Write the colour-only result separately from the disclosed reference pH and numerical work. State the illustrative scale, neglected indicator load, ideal reference conditions and lack of measured dye kinetics or universal colour calibration. Do not claim a chemical identity from matching colour.
