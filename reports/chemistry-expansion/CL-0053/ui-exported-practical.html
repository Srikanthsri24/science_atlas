Compare sieving and filtration

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · CL-0053 · Class 4 editorial investigation

Aim

Compare matched wet sieving and ordinary fine filtration. Record which reference grains pass or remain, and distinguish dissolved salt from undissolved particles.

Apparatus and model

Two independent equal prepared feeds, a reference welded sieve, a reference fine filter, matched metered feed controllers and separate receivers. Fine sand is represented by rigid 150 µm grains and gravel by 3000 µm grains. Adjustable ideal openings are not manufacturer grades. Salted feeds are already prepared at 1 g dissolved salt per 100 mL solution; no volume-of-dissolution calculation is implied. Equal-diameter grains are retained under the model's zero-clearance policy. The prepared liquid passes both reference separators. Colour tags are explanatory; ordinary salt solution is colourless. Chosen dry separator tares are 20 g for the sieve and 2 g for the filter support. Readings are dry-equivalent reference masses, not measured wet-paper masses.

Prediction

With matched feeds,I expect the coarse sieve to retain gravel but pass fine sand. The finer reference filter should retain both undissolved grains. Dissolved salt should remain in both collected liquid streams.

Procedure

  1. Record the prepared feed volume and profile per arm. Each arm has its own full feed; do not divide one feed between the two stations.
  2. Choose sieve and filter openings. Compare them with the 150 µm and 3000 µm reference diameters in the magnified view.
  3. Start the matched metered feeds and pause at physical seconds. The 120 s guide compresses 60 physical seconds into 90 moving seconds plus setup/conclusion holds; manual 120 s playback spans 90 physical seconds.
  4. Record remaining feed, dry-equivalent retained grains, passed grains, collected liquid and dissolved salt. Check each component separately.
  5. Repeat with a larger filter opening, a sieve large enough to pass gravel, and a salt-free feed. At zero collection, concentration is undefined.
  6. Apply the substitutions below. For a real dry residue measurement, dry the residue and subtract the dry empty separator tare; absorbed solution and wet-paper mass are omitted here.
  7. Conclude which separation worked for each reference grain and whether dissolved salt was removed. Grain-free filtrate is not a drinking-safety result.

Result and conclusion

Matched reference trials retained15g in the sieve and25g in the filter. Each collected100mL prepared liquid with1g dissolved salt. Coarser openings changed grain passage;ordinary particle separation did not remove the dissolved salt. Dry tare subtraction and separate grain,liquid andsalt budgets agreed with the observations. A grain-free filtrate was not a drinking-safety result.

Precautions and limitations

The uniform 60 s transfer is prescribed, not a hydraulic-rate prediction. Ideal straight openings, rigid reference grains, uniform feeds, immediate drainage and zero apparatus liquid holdup are assumed. Settling, clogging, bridging, capacity, capillary wetting, adsorption and selective membranes are omitted. Bulk grain packing and enlarged particle views are explanatory. Dry-equivalent residues exclude absorbed solution. This model does not establish drinking safety, manufacturer performance, physical calibration, official board alignment or independent expert certification.
