Compare particle arrangements in solid, liquid and gas

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs · Class 3 · CL-0025

Aim

Compare arrangement, spacing, motion and changes in close neighbours using three separate 64-particle models.

Apparatus and principle

Three schematic particle chambers, selected-particle marker, timer, motion trail and numerical readings. Equal-radius spheres represent particles; they are not photographs or actual molecular sizes. Fixed-site vibration illustrates a crystalline solid. Dense and dilute hard-sphere samples illustrate close rearrangement and freer gas motion. The dense region stands in for liquid cohesion, which is not calculated.

Prediction

I expect fixed-site vibration in the solid example, close particles changing neighbours in the liquid analogy, and freer motion with wider gaps in the gas model.

Procedure

  1. Select the solid example and particle 1. Record initial evidence, run 30 presentation seconds, pause and record again.
  2. Repeat for the liquid model, keeping the tracked particle number and sampling times comparable within that sample.
  3. Repeat for the gas model. Record all 64 particles in the count, even when a 2D projection overlaps.
  4. Observe whether the marked particle vibrates about a site or changes location. Use its actual path and close-neighbour count as evidence.
  5. Distinguish net displacement from distance travelled. Explain why the 2D projection can overlap while the 3D particles remain separated.
  6. Write a result comparing arrangement and motion. State that sample sizes/speeds are prescribed and no physical phase transition was simulated.

Result and conclusion

All three samples kept 64 equal-radius representative particles. The crystalline example vibrated around fixed sites. The dense liquid analogy changed close neighbours, while the dilute gas explored its larger region. Net displacement differs from path length. These are prescribed model samples in arbitrary units; selecting a state did not simulate heating or liquid cohesion.

Precautions and limitations

Compare one sample at a time and pause before recording. The 2D view is a front projection with 16 tracers emphasized, not a physical thin slice; depth overlaps are possible. The 3D view shows all 64 particles; its optional gold outline locates a tracked particle hidden by the sample. Views may be fitted at different zooms; sphere radius remains 0.45 u. Molecular time/length/energy are arbitrary. Fixed sites describe a crystalline example, not every solid. Hard spheres with reflecting prescribed regions do not solve attractive forces, liquid cohesion, evaporation, free-surface motion, temperature or phase equilibrium. A close-neighbour threshold is a measurement rule, not a chemical bond. Sample changes are not heating/cooling experiments. Internal engineering checks do not imply independent expert or official board certification.
