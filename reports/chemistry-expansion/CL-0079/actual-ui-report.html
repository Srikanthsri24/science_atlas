CL-0079 · Temperature and saturation

Aim,apparatus and procedure

Two reference sample vessels,thermostatic baths,temperature probes,known water and solute masses. Generic chosen solubility curves;not calibrated chemical data.

  1. Aim: compare the saturation limits of equal reference mixtures at two controlled temperatures.
  2. Prepare two separate samples with the same water mass and solute mass. The simulation begins with each sample equilibrated at25°C;do not interpret this as instantaneous preparation.
  3. Choose two bath temperatures. Record the measured sample temperatures,not just the bath settings. Keep water mass,solute identity and mixing assumptions the same.
  4. Watch material dissolve or grow as sample temperature changes. Solid residue during a transient does not by itself establish saturation. The chosen model assumes no delayed nucleation.
  5. Wait until each sample is nearly steady: temperature within0.01°C of its bath and dissolved mass within0.001g of its instantaneous target. These are reference observation criteria,not instrument accuracy.
  6. Record both temperatures,dissolved and undissolved masses,time and whether excess solid remains. A clear sample with all material dissolved establishes only that this loading is below or at its limit.
  7. For a nearly steady sample with excess solid,calculate solubility as100 × dissolved solute mass / water mass. Never divide by total solution mass.
  8. Compare the two samples,repeat with another chosen trend,and write an evidence-based conclusion. Not all solids become more soluble on heating.

Prediction and plan

Keep water and solute masses equal in both samples. Compare measured sample temperatures and wait for near-steady samples with excess solid before estimating solubility.
