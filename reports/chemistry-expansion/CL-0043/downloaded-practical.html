Recover dissolved salt by evaporation

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · CL-0043 · Class 4 editorial investigation

Aim

Observe concentration and crystal growth as water leaves a salt solution. Account for dissolved salt, crystalline salt, remaining water and wet residue separately.

Apparatus and model

Virtual shallow evaporation dish, balance readings and a fixed 25 °C reference environment. Start with 100 g water and 5–25 g dissolved sodium chloride. The chosen effective declining drying law is W=100exp(−kt)g, with k=0.0002–0.0012s⁻¹. Reference capacity is 0.36 g salt per gram water. Equilibrium crystallization is instantaneous; no nucleation delay or supersaturation kinetics are solved. Water transfer to air is invisible; illustrative vapour glyphs are not a cloud of steam.

Prediction

Water should leave the open dish while salt stays. The solution should concentrate before crystals appear. I will distinguish remaining water, dissolved salt, crystal salt and wet contents.

Procedure

  1. Prepare the chosen salt mass fully dissolved in 100 g water. Record the initial state and prediction.
  2. Begin the reference drying run. Record water remaining and solution concentration before crystals appear.
  3. Observe the calculated saturation onset. Compare initial salt with the shrinking dissolved capacity.
  4. Record a later state with crystals and remaining mother liquor. Distinguish solid salt mass from wet contents of the dish.
  5. Repeat with a fresh sample, changing only the chosen drying coefficient or starting salt mass.
  6. Apply the water, salt and complete mass balances. Write a result and conclusion from the recorded observations.
