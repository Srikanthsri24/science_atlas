Compare a solution and a suspension

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · Class 3 · CL-0032

Aim

Compare a prepared salt solution with insoluble model powder settling in the same starting water. Distinguish dissolved, suspended and settled material.

Apparatus and principle

Two equal-area model cells,100 mL starting water and 2 g material in each, a clock and settling ruler. Cell A contains already dissolved salt. Cell B contains insoluble ideal spherical particles. The model assumes dilute Stokes settling and a growing sediment bed with chosen packing0.6. Particle radius5–15 µm, reference 20°C, densities2700 and1000kg/m³ and viscosity0.001Pa s are model/reference values, not calibrated chalk measurements.

Prediction

QA: prepared salt should remain dissolved, while insoluble model powder settles. At the same time,5µm particles should settle more slowly than10µm particles.

Procedure

  1. Record both prepared samples at zero time. Confirm2 g dissolved salt in A and 2 g suspended powder in B.
  2. Start with10 µm radius. Pause at matching times and record suspended and settled mass, clear-layer thickness and sediment depth.
  3. Check suspended plus settled powder equals2 g and both whole samples retain102 g.
  4. Inspect the lower section. The thin bed has a physical model depth; its close-up is labelled.
  5. Prepare a fresh 5 µm suspension and repeat the same observation time. Compare the square-of-radius relationship and completion times.
  6. Write a conclusion supported by observations and calculations. State the ideal-model limitations.

Precautions and limitations

Compare the same elapsed time in fresh samples when changing size. Salt is already dissolved; this is not a dissolution-rate comparison. Uniform monodisperse spherical particles and a low-Reynolds-number dilute suspension are assumed. No Brownian diffusion, aggregation, fluid return flow, irregular shapes, optical scattering or resolved ions are simulated. Enlarged markers show representative paths, not physical particle counts; aggregate masses come from a continuous initial-height distribution. Sediment packing0.6 is chosen. Suspension volume includes solid displacement; solution geometry shows reference starting-water volume without predicting final dissolved-solution volume. The2D lower-section view uses8× vertical magnification, explicitly labelled;3D close-up uses camera magnification. All values are simulated evidence, not independent expert or official-board certification.
