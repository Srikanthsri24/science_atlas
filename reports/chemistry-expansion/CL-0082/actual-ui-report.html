CL-0082 · Separate salt,sand and iron in stages

Aim,apparatus and procedure

Prepared mixture,covered magnet and collection tray,water reservoirs,filter assembly,combined-filtrate dish,separate drying and reference balance readings.

  1. Aim: separate a prepared iron,sand and soluble-salt mixture,and compare product recovery with purity.
  2. Record all initial solid masses and reserve the initial water and each fresh wash. The system ledger includes reserved water from the start.
  3. Choose magnet passes and the reference capture/co-pickup fractions. Collect the covered magnet product dry. Mechanical co-pickup is not magnetic attraction of salt or sand.
  4. Add the initial water and dissolve salt to the chosen capacity. Ordinary filtration retains iron,sand and any undissolved salt. Retained mother liquor carries additional dissolved salt.
  5. For each wash,add the selected fresh water,mix,and drain separately. Collect each filtrate with the salt solution. Excess undissolved salt requires a capacity calculation;simple geometric dilution is valid only when it is all dissolved.
  6. Dry the retained cake and combined filtrate separately to the prescribed reference endpoint. The model assumes a cooled dry weighing condition there;the procedure clock is not an actual drying-time guarantee.
  7. Record dry gross readings and subtract the stated tare. Label a total-product mass recovery as apparent when contaminants are present. A balance reading alone does not identify product composition.
  8. Use known reference component inventories to calculate recovery and purity separately. Explain why washing removes soluble contamination but does not remove iron left with the sand.
  9. Check iron,sand,salt and water balances separately. Write aim,apparatus,method,observations,formula substitution,result,conclusion and limitations.

Prediction and plan

Compare magnet pass count and mechanical co-pickup separately. Keep known initial masses. Record each transfer,then compare zero and repeated washing. Separate dry product weight from known component recovery and purity.
