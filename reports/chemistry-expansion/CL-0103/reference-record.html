Aim and prediction

Compare <A> & <B>

Apparatus and principle

Two matched virtual peroxide preparations with zeroed freely moving gas syringes. 2H₂O₂ →2H₂O + O₂. Independently assumed first-order coefficients;isobaric ideal-gas expansion. Constants:R8.314kPa L mol⁻¹ K⁻¹;peroxide34.014,water18.015,oxygen31.998,air28.97g/mol;initial dry-air oxygen fraction0.2095;initial liquid density1g/mL;flask100mL;syringe150mL.

Procedure and recording instructions

  1. Write an aim: compare total gas collected and current rate in two matched virtual preparations. Predict whether one cell will always have the greater rate.
  2. Record liquid volume, initial concentration, both assumed coefficients, gas temperature and external pressure. Keep preparations matched; zero concentration is a water control.
  3. Start with zero syringe increments. Run the guide, pause at several model times, and record each paired observation. The 100-second moving part represents 180 model seconds; read the model clock rather than treating guide time as elapsed reaction time.
  4. Complete the paired state, gas and mass tables. Record the gas increment separately from original headspace. The syringe contains a gas mixture; generated oxygen amount refers to the whole connected gas space.
  5. Apply n₀=C₀V/1000, n_left=n₀exp(−kt), n_O₂=(n₀−n_left)/2 and ΔV=n_O₂RT/P. Show values, units and results. Compare the current gas slope with the mean gas increment divided by elapsed time.
  6. Repeat with equal, reversed and zero coefficients. Compare predictions at the calculated rate-crossover time where accessible. A greater accumulated gas volume does not guarantee a greater current rate.
  7. Write the result using recorded evidence, then explain depletion and conservation. Distinguish your observations from the assumed first-order model. Add limitations and a separate student conclusion.
