Carbonate surface and defined acid exposure — practical record

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · CL-0111

Aim and prediction

Compare equal cumulative exposure with matched controls.

Apparatus and principle

Matched finite feed reservoirs, contact and bypass streams, carbonate slabs, runoff collectors and ideal dry-gas capture accounts. CaCO₃ + 2 H⁺ → Ca²⁺ + CO₂ + H₂O; nitrate counterions remain in solution.

Procedure and recording instructions

  1. Write an aim and prediction. Compare water, half-strength acid, selected acid and a no-specimen reagent blank using the same exposure volume.
  2. Record initial mass, planar area, acid-equivalent concentration, temperature, pressure, contact fraction and gas-capture fraction before changing any control.
  3. Pause at a selected cumulative exposure and record all four cells as one trial. Repeat at several exposure volumes; keep the other controls fixed for a fair comparison.
  4. Copy solid mass, removed depth, calcium, acid remaining and dry-gas equivalent into the corresponding observation tables with units. Record what was visible separately from model calculations.
  5. Apply the 2:1 acid-to-carbonate ratio, identify the limiting reactant, substitute the recorded values and calculate mass loss and gas equivalents. Check mass and aqueous-charge residuals.
  6. Explain the difference between specimen loss and whole-apparatus loss: dissolved calcium stays in runoff, captured gas stays inside, and only escaped gas leaves the accounted apparatus.
  7. Write a result and conclusion supported by your trials. State that this defined acid reference does not predict natural-rain pH, monument erosion rate or equilibrium carbonate dissolution.

Result and conclusion

Defined accessible acid capacity limits carbonate conversion; specimen loss differs from escaped gas mass. This is not measured environmental weathering.

Precautions and interpretation limits

  • A defined nitric-acid-equivalent teaching preparation, not measured rainwater. Acid capacity is supplied explicitly and is not inferred from pH.
  • Instantaneous stoichiometric reaction endpoint with ideal complete CO₂ stripping. Dissolved carbonate species, atmospheric carbonic-acid weathering, gypsum and reaction kinetics are omitted.
  • Uniform thinning at fixed planar area; calcite reference density 2.71 g/mL. The surface view may require a labelled magnification. No roughness or pitting law is predicted.
  • Prepared solution density is selected as 1 g/mL. Carrier mass excludes acid solute; gas volume is a dry ideal-gas equivalent at selected temperature and pressure.
  • Each cell has its own finite 1000 mL stock. The 120-second sequence stages laboratory delivery and interpretation, not elapsed environmental weathering time.
  • The blank has no carbonate specimen. Required removal volume is undefined for the blank, water control or zero contact. A computed requirement may exceed available stock.
  • Virtual investigation: numerical outputs are model results, not experimental measurements. A classroom activity involving acids requires a teacher-approved procedure and suitable protection.
