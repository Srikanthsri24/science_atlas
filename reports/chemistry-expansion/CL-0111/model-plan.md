# CL-0111 — Follow acid-rain effects on a carbonate surface

Status: in progress. Numerical core and downloadable practical-report generator implemented; 2D/3D viewers, UI integration and browser review remain outstanding. This lab is not counted completed.

Compare a carbonate specimen exposed to a defined acid-equivalent reference with a matched control. The visual should show metered rain/exposure, retained solid, dissolved calcium and a separately accounted carbon dioxide channel. Preserve both specimen mass and a whole-apparatus material/charge account.

Do not infer cumulative acid capacity from rain pH alone or call concentrated laboratory acid realistic rainfall. Natural carbonic-acid weathering and pollutant-derived acidity require distinct explanations. A possible first model is a clearly selected dilute nitric-acid-equivalent teaching preparation with finite total exposure, not an atmospheric chemistry model or calibrated stone-weathering law.

Candidate net reaction: CaCO3 + 2 H+ → Ca2+ + CO2 + H2O. If nitric-acid reference is selected, retain nitrate counterions and unreacted acid. Distinguish released/captured gas, remaining feed and generated water rather than silently losing material. Initial prepared solution mass must exclude solutes from the carrier to prevent double counting. Verify exact constants and assumptions before implementation.

Rain-like acid amounts may produce a loss too small to see at whole-specimen scale. Use an explicitly labelled magnified loss section and quantitative mass/depth measurements. Any large-volume or repeated-exposure staging must show its finite supplied stock and must not be presented as real elapsed weathering time. Gypsum crusts, pits, roughness evolution, atmospheric mixtures, dissolved carbonate speciation and pH trajectories are not predicted by a simple stoichiometric reference.

Required practical: aim, controlled variables, initial mass, selected acid-equivalent concentration and exposure volume, dose-by-dose observation table, limiting-reactant substitution, carbonate mass lost, remaining mass, calcium/gas/material accounts and a conclusion limited to the reference. Plan an original ≥60-second 2D/3D explanation with visible exposure and magnified loss, then independent numerical/report/geometry checks and actual browser review.
