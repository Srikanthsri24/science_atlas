Use a balance to compare sample masses

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs · Class 3 · CL-0027

Aim

Compare a sample with known reference masses using equal arms and distinguish level balance from a moving or stopped beam.

Apparatus and principle

Ideal beam balance, equal 12 cm arms, equal 15 g pans, adjustable sample and reference loads. A beam centre of mass below the pivot restores it toward level. Equal masses give equal gravitational torques at level. This model reveals input masses for learning; it is not a blind calibrated measurement.

Prediction

QA trial: the 50 g sample should lower the left pan against a 40 g reference; equal 50 g loads should remain level.

Procedure

  1. Set the sample to 50 g and reference to 40 g. Reset and release from level.
  2. Observe which pan descends. Pause and record time, angle, loads and status. Contact with a stop is not a mass reading.
  3. Reset and repeat with 60 g, then 49 g reference. Compare the directions and settling.
  4. Reset with 50 g on both sides. Confirm a level resting beam and calculate the sample mass using the equal-arm rule.
  5. Change gravity while keeping both masses fixed. Compare dynamics and distinguish mass in g from force in N.
  6. Write a result supported by your records and one model limitation.

Result and conclusion

QA report: equal 50 g loads gave a level beam and equal 0.058860000 N m torques. By m_sample = m_reference × L_right/L_left, 50 × 0.120/0.120 = 50 g. Unequal loads can settle at a nonzero angle or contact a stop; neither establishes level balance. Gravity changes forces, while equal-arm mass comparison remains unchanged. The model excludes pan swing, bearing friction, buoyancy and instrument calibration.

Precautions and limitations

Reset before every changed load. Wait for rest and level before applying the mass comparison rule. Do not use a travel stop as evidence of balance. Pans remain vertical by constraint; pan swing, bearing friction, buoyancy and instrument calibration are excluded. A rigid beam and linear damping are assumed. This is simulated evidence and internal engineering review does not imply independent expert or official board certification.
