Compare filter papers with different pore sizes

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · CL-0042 · Class 4 editorial investigation

Aim

Compare collected water and particle capture after equal times using matched ideal filters with different openings.

Apparatus and reference sample

Two independent filter modules and receivers. Each starts with 100 mL reference water plus 0.02 g each of 4, 12 and 30 µm insoluble reference spheres. Same area 10 cm², thickness 0.2 mm, porosity 0.4 and effective path factor 5. These are chosen clean capillary-bundle models, not measured paper grades. Nominal particle retention of real fibrous paper is not a geometric pore diameter.

Prediction

For equal fresh samples and elapsed time, the wider ideal opening should collect more water but pass more of the mixed reference solids. Equal filters should give equal results.

Procedure

  1. Prepare equal fresh samples. Keep all settings except ideal opening diameter the same.
  2. Record initial water and solid masses before starting both modules together.
  3. Pause at a chosen physical time and record collected water, current flow, captured solids and receiver solids for both modules.
  4. Account for each particle size separately. Compare4,12 and30µm diameters with each opening.
  5. Calculate water collected, mean rate and complete mass balance. Teacher extension: derive permeability, pressure and falling-head rate.
  6. Write a conclusion supported by paired observations. Distinguish captured solids from upstream solids and clear appearance from safety.

Precautions and limitations

Use equal fresh samples and equal elapsed physical time. In this ideal model wider openings increase clean conductance at fixed material parameters; real paper flow also depends on thickness, architecture and other properties. Rigid spheres at or above the ideal opening are retained. Real depth retention has a distribution, not a perfect switch. Well mixing suppresses settling. Constant clean permeability neglects cake, blocking, adsorption, wetting transients, capillarity and leaks. Hydraulic head omits 0.022222 mL initial solid displacement against 100 mL water, although solid volume is accounted separately in the visualization. Finite time leaves upstream water and solids. Captured solids are not dried or weighed experimental yield. No measured efficiency, drinking-water safety or official curriculum verification is certified.
