Separate large and small grains with a sieve

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs · CL-0015 · Class 2

Aim

Compare how opening size affects separation of dry model specimens and account for all material.

Apparatus and principle

Ideal circular-opening sieve, collector, adjustable shaking and timer; 30 spherical model specimens, five of each diameter 2–7 mm. Only diameters strictly smaller than the aperture can pass.

Prediction

Only pieces smaller than an opening should pass. A larger opening should collect more size groups.

Procedure

  1. Inspect the six diameter groups and predict which fit a 3 mm opening.
  2. Record at 0 s, run shaking and record after 12 model seconds.
  3. Repeat with 5 mm and 8 mm apertures at equal agitation and duration.
  4. Try an aperture equal to a specimen diameter; record the boundary outcome.
  5. Try agitation 0 as a control; compare intermediate in-transit and final masses.
  6. Use the table to explain separation and one limitation of ideal spherical specimens.

Result and conclusion

At 12 s and shaking 1, openings of 3, 5 and 8 mm collect 5, 15 and 30 specimens. At 5 mm and shaking 0.25, only 40% of passable mass has been recovered after 12 s. All trials preserve 30 specimens and 5.124723016 g. This ideal model excludes real grain shape and clogging.

Precautions and limitations

Use dry specimens and equal conditions in comparisons. Do not taste laboratory material. This is an ideal spherical, circular-opening model with chosen exposure schedules and fixed density, not calibrated grain-flow kinetics. Shape, orientation, aperture tolerances, collision, clogging, breakage and electrostatic effects are excluded. Agitation 0 leaves all pieces unexposed in this chosen schedule. Equal diameters are retained by the strict passage rule; real clearances require tolerance. No substance changes identity or mass during separation. Internal engineering checks do not imply independent expert or official board certification.
