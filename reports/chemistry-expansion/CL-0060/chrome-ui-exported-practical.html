Compare crystal growth during fast and slow cooling

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · CL-0060 · Class 5 editorial investigation

Aim

Investigate how two prescribed cooling durations affect supersaturation, effective growth sites and equivalent crystal size for equal initial solutions.

Apparatus and assumptions

Two covered virtual potassium-nitrate solutions start at 80°C with no crystals. Water and salt are conserved. Linear cooling ends at 20°C and then holds. The solubility reference is a coarse piecewise interpolation on a water-mass basis. Effective site formation and surface growth use chosen teaching laws; they are not calibrated material kinetics or literal crystal counts. No evaporation or heat-balance solver.

Prediction

Equal initial masses; compare at equal final temperature. <check>

Procedure

  1. Record equal water and dissolved salt masses, fast duration and slow multiplier. Keep growth flux, site coefficient and threshold matched.
  2. Predict the effect of cooling duration. Both samples begin clear at 80°C.
  3. Run and pause at several elapsed times. Record both temperatures as well as dissolved and solid inventories.
  4. Compare after both programs have reached 20°C, allowing further growth if needed. Equal elapsed time during cooling does not imply equal temperature.
  5. Record effective sites, equivalent radius and current supersaturation. Distinguish cumulative mass from instantaneous growth rate.
  6. Check salt and water closure; substitute values with units. State the observed model result and its limitations.

Result, conclusion and limitations

Salt is conserved. Chosen rates are illustrative; effective sites are not literal counts.

Interpretation and precautions

Virtual investigation, not an unsupervised chemical preparation. Effective sites may be fractional. Equivalent spherical size and area do not predict actual habit or a measured size distribution. Cooling is prescribed, not a thermal calculation. All nucleus mass is included in the solid inventory. The chosen coefficients are not measured nucleation or growth constants. Numerical integration uses steps no larger than 0.05 s; finite-step convergence is checked separately. No official-board, physical-device or independent-expert certification is claimed.
