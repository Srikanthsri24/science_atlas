Identify a suitable container for a liquid

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs · Class 2 · CL-0024

Aim

Choose a container for holding or carrying water using evidence from equal-volume upright and tilted trials.

Apparatus and principle

Matched cylindrical open tumbler, watertight lidded jar and open cup with a base hole; water, measured-volume readings, tilting stand, timer and catch tray. Water has a horizontal free surface when at rest. An open rim or a hole can provide an escape path.

Prediction

The open tumbler should hold water upright, but the sealed jar should retain it when tilted. The holed cup should lose water even upright.

Procedure

  1. Select 150 mL and an upright target of 0°. Reset each trial and compare the three containers at 0 s and 30 s.
  2. Record the container, starting volume, target angle, actual angle, elapsed time, retained water and both loss channels. Add an observation describing the water path.
  3. Keep the initial amount fixed. Repeat for a target of 90°; the stand tilts gradually over 20 s and then holds.
  4. Pause at intermediate times to see when water first reaches the lowest rim or leaves the base hole.
  5. Check retained + rim spill + base leakage against starting water. Compare only matched starting conditions.
  6. Choose an appropriate container for an upright desk and for carrying while tilted. Write a conclusion from your recorded evidence.

Result and conclusion

Matched 150 mL trials show that the ideal sealed jar retains water at 90°. The open tumbler spills through its rim; the holed cup also leaks through its base. Choose a container for the intended task and check its seal. These are slow-tilt model results; real sloshing and small-hole capillary effects are excluded.

Precautions and limitations

Reset between trials and change one condition at a time. This is a slow-tilt, quasistatic model without sloshing, surface tension, viscosity beyond the chosen discharge factor, jet-flight inventory or seal deformation. The holed cup is open to air. The sealed container has a perfect lid and no hole. Leakage timing is illustrative and is not a prediction for every physical cup. Use safe water containers for any supervised physical comparison; do not assume real lids are watertight. Internal engineering checks do not imply independent expert certification or verified board mapping.
