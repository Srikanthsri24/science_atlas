Coarse and fine sugar: equal mass, different area

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · Class 3 · CL-0030

Aim

Compare the time to dissolve 9 g when the same 10 g sample is divided into different numbers of equal cubic model grains.

Apparatus and principle

Two reference 200 mL water cells at 20 °C, permeable grain supports, matched sugar masses and a common clock. More smaller grains expose more initial area. The chosen coefficient and reference solubility stay fixed. This is a shrinking-grain model, not a calibrated prediction for real sugar crystals.

Prediction

QA: equal 10 g samples should dissolve at different rates because smaller grains have greater total exposed initial area.

Procedure

  1. Choose 8 grains in A and 512 in B. Check 10 g sugar, 200 mL water, 20 °C and identical mixing assumptions in both cells.
  2. Record initial grain sides and total exposed areas. Inspect both at the common magnification.
  3. Start together and pause at matched model times. Record solid and dissolved masses and whether 9 g has dissolved.
  4. Substitute the recorded counts into a0 = cube-root[10/(1.59 N)] and A0 = 6Na0². Calculate the area and target-time ratios.
  5. Repeat with equal grain counts and exchange A and B. Use observations to write a result and a model limitation.

Result and conclusion

QA result: at 60 model seconds, the 8-grain sample had dissolved 5.669850 g and the 512-grain sample 9.999511 g. Equal 8/8 counts matched, and exchanging counts exchanged the 30-second outcomes. A0_B/A0_A = cube-root(512/8) = 4, and the predicted target-time ratio is also 4. Solid plus dissolved remained 10 g. These are simulated values under a chosen cubic-grain and ideal-mixing model, not calibrated physical timings.

Precautions and limitations

Compare equal masses at equal times or at the same dissolved target. Do not confuse grain count with mass or initial area with every later rate. Reference water mass is 200 g, solid density 1.59 g/cm³ and capacity 408 g at 20 °C. The coefficient is chosen, not calibrated. Cubic grains, ideal bulk mixing, constant reference water geometry and negligible support obstruction are assumptions. Clumping, fragmentation, crystal facets, flow, temperature change and solution-volume change are excluded. 2D shows a labelled front slice; 3D contains every grain. Inspection shows one grain at a common magnification and its initial wireframe. All values are simulated evidence.
