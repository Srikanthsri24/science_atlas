# CL-0030 — Fine versus coarse sugar

In progress, not reviewed or complete. Both samples have 10 g sugar, 200 mL reference water at 20 °C, density 1.59 g/cm³, reference capacity 408 g and chosen coefficient 0.003 g/(cm² s). Only the number and therefore size of equal cubic grains changes. The default comparison is 8 grains against 512, with the existing supported counts 8, 64, 216, 512 and 1000. Model grains are not molecules or a crystallographic unit-cell claim.

The reviewed CL-0022 autonomous solver is reused unchanged. Since k enters solely as a time multiplier, evaluation at k_eval = factor × k_model and t_eval = t_model/factor gives the same state. A reference setting with coefficient 0.009 is evaluated at one third of the 0–300 s model time, keeping evaluation inside the reference solver's 120 s domain. Returned rates use the actual 0.003 coefficient and completion times are converted back. Independent comparisons with the original zero-stirring model and RK4 integrations validate this exact computational rescaling; it is not a different displayed experiment.

At fixed mass and density, a0 = cube-root[M0/(rho N)], so initial total area 6Na0² scales as cube-root(N). With matched coefficient/capacity and the same dissolved fraction, target time scales inversely with cube-root(N). The default 64-fold count difference therefore gives fourfold initial area and fourfold target-time ratio. This is not a claim that later instantaneous rates remain in that ratio after the faster sample dissolves. Initial states are imposed exactly and every state conserves 10 g sugar.
