Neutralization calorimeter · practical record

Science Atlas360° · VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited

Aim,hypothesis and plan

Compare staged delivery <and> thermal evidence.

Apparatus,procedure and precautions

  1. Aim: investigate temperature change during strong-acid/strong-base neutralization and distinguish reaction extent from temperature evidence.
  2. Apparatus: finite prepared acid reservoir with metered delivery,insulated cup containing prepared base,stirring rod,temperature probe,volume scale and temperature-versus-delivered-volume trace. This virtual activity is not a home mixing instruction.
  3. Before delivery,record both initial volumes,concentrations,the common initial temperature and cup heat capacity. Keep these fixed while comparing different delivery fractions.
  4. Record the zero-delivery control. Meter acid into the cup in stages. At every observation,record delivered and remaining acid volumes,solution volume,temperature and analytical excess. The reservoir and cup together retain the original additive volume.
  5. Observe the well-mixed solution and temperature trace. The ideal model mixes/reacts immediately at each controlled delivered-volume state;stirring/flow animation is explanatory,not measured kinetics.
  6. Calculate initial/delivered amounts in moles using concentration in mol/L and volumes converted from mL. Use the smaller delivered acid/base amount as reaction extent for the1:1 reaction.
  7. Compute the signed reaction heat and positive heat absorbed by both solution and cup. Apply Q_released = (m c + C_cup) ΔT,then show all substitutions with units.
  8. Compare the cup-corrected reconstructed molar enthalpy with the solution-only apparent estimate. At zero reacted amount,write not defined rather than dividing by zero. The reconstructed value repeats the chosen model parameter;it is not an independent experimental determination.
  9. Check whether the calculated equivalence volume is reachable with available acid. Temperature can fall after equivalence as additional acid increases heat capacity;the ideal model contains no environmental heat loss.
  10. Write a separate result and conclusion:state whether warming occurred,which analytical reagent remains,and the limits of the ideal calorimeter model. Warming alone does not prove a neutral endpoint or chemical identity.
