Compare salt and sand in water

Science Atlas 360° · VisionariesAI Labs · CL-0013 · Class 2

Aim

Compare dissolution under equal starting water volume, added mass, stirring and model time; show that dissolved material still contributes to mass.

Apparatus and principle

Transparent beaker, starting water, salt or sand, stirring rod, model timer and mass display. Salt has an approximate fixed-temperature capacity; sand dissolution is neglected over this classroom timescale.

Prediction

Salt will dissolve; sand will remain solid. More water may dissolve more salt.

Procedure

  1. Set 20 g material, 100 mL starting water and stirring 1. Record at 0 s.
  2. Run the salt experiment to 120 model seconds and record. Repeat with sand at the same settings.
  3. Compare salt at stirring 0 and 1 at equal times; stirring changes the chosen rate, not the capacity.
  4. Test 50 g salt in 100 mL, then 250 mL. Record remaining solid and distinguish finite-time results from equilibrium.
  5. Use 0 g as a control. Use recorded evidence to write a result and a model limitation.

Result and conclusion

At 120 s, 20 g salt in 100 mL leaves 0.049575 g, while sand leaves 20 g. With 50 g salt in 100 mL, 14.089235 g remains. Total mass is conserved. Rates are chosen, not measured kinetics.

Precautions and limitations

Keep variables equal when comparing materials. Do not taste laboratory samples. Real experiments require measured masses and a balance; these values are calculated from a model. Temperature is fixed at 25 °C; NaCl capacity is approximated as 0.36 g per mL starting water. Sand dissolution is neglected, not universally impossible. The first-order kinetic rate is a chosen teaching parameter, not a measured prediction. Water density is approximated as 1 g/mL. Evaporation, heat, final solution volume changes and fluid flow are excluded. Crystal blocks and dissolved markers are representative display aids, not counted atoms or resolved grain dynamics. Manual model time runs 6× faster. Review status: internal engineering checks; class placement is editorial.
