Selected oxide neutralization: acid and alkali demand

VisionariesAI Labs Private Limited · CL-0125

Aim

Compare finite CuO/HCl neutralization with separate closed dissolved-carbon/NaOH dosing using stock, element, charge and equilibrium accounts.

Apparatus

Virtual CuO/acid vessel, independent closed-carbon reference vessel, metered20mL stock syringe, mass/volume inputs and ideal right-station pH/speciation readout.

Safety

Virtual only. Real copper-compound, acid and alkali work requires teacher supervision, protective equipment and approved waste handling. No flame or ingestion activity is provided.

Prediction and plan

Compare finite CuO acid demand with independent closed-carbon alkali demand. Change one input at a time and retain both station accounts.

Procedure

  1. Predict why a selected basic metal oxide consumes acid and an acidic non-metal oxide responds to alkali. Do not extend this result to neutral or amphoteric oxides.
  2. Record CuO mass and HCl concentration/volume. Use m/M and cV/2 to identify the finite CuO reaction capacity before advancing controlled extent.
  3. Keep the closed carbon reference separate. Record its analytical carbon amount, initial volume, NaOH stock concentration/volume and delivered fraction.
  4. Advance each independent station. Observe CuO remaining and acid consumed on the left; read added stock volume, changing carbon distribution and ideal pH on the right.
  5. Pause and record the four observation tables. Track undelivered NaOH in its stock reservoir; the mixed-solution volume includes delivered stock.
  6. Test no-contact/no-CuO/no-acid controls, carbon-free water/NaOH controls and one/two base-equivalent regions. A zero-carbon case has no carbon-species amounts or defined equivalence ratio.
  7. Apply the substitutions. Advanced right-station calculations solve water and diprotic charge balance at25°C; CO2* includes CO2(aq) and H2CO3. Left pH is unavailable without Cu(II) hydrolysis/complexation.
  8. Conclude from finite titrant consumption and species accounts. State the ideal-concentration, isothermal, closed-carbon and controlled-extent assumptions; guide time does not measure reaction rates.

Precautions and limitations

Only selected CuO/HCl and separate closed preloaded dissolved-carbon/NaOH references are modelled. Not all metal oxides are basic or non-metal oxides acidic. Cu(II) hydrolysis/complexation are excluded: left pH unavailable. Right model uses ideal concentration-form K1=4.3e-7,K2=4.7e-11,Kw=1e-14 at25°C; CO2* is the combined aqueousCO2/H2CO3 neutral pool. No gas absorption/headspace, atmosphere exchange, inter-station mixing, kinetic/thermal/activity or calibrated indicator solver. Reference liquid volumes; CuO grains are normalized stock aids.
